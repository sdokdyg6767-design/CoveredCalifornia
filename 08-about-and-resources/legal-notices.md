# Serving a Legal Notice (Service of Process)

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/legal-notices
- **Covered California section:** About / Legal
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page tells anyone serving legal documents on Covered California how to do so validly. Because of a hybrid workplace with limited staff on site, Covered California does **not** accept personal service. Service must be made **both** by email and by mail.

## Key Facts

- **Personal service is not accepted.** "Instead of personal service, Covered California will accept service of process of legal documents by email and mail."
- **Email service address:** **RecordsCustodian@covered.ca.gov**
- **Mail service address:** Office of Legal Affairs, 1601 Exposition Blvd., Sacramento, CA 95815
- **Dual service is required:** "If serving documents by email, please also mail those documents to the address listed below. This will ensure Covered California receives them."
- **Confirmation of receipt:** Sent to the sender by return email. **"If you don't receive a confirmation of receipt, service of process has not been effected by Covered California."**
- **After-hours rule:** Documents submitted **after 5 p.m., on a weekend, or on a state holiday** are construed to have been presented on the **next business day at 9 a.m.**
- **Covered California reserves the right to reject service of process where a defect in service is found.**

## Details

### Service by Email

Legal documents must be emailed to **RecordsCustodian@covered.ca.gov**. Confirmation of receipt and acceptance of service is delivered to the sender via return email.

The operative warning: without that confirmation email, service has not been effected. Email alone is therefore insufficient — the same documents must also be mailed.

### Service by Mail

Mail documents to:

> Office of Legal Affairs
> 1601 Exposition Blvd.
> Sacramento, CA 95815

The page reiterates that you should **also** email the legal documents, so that confirmation of receipt and acceptance of service can be returned to you by email.

### Defensibility

Covered California states plainly that it "reserves the right to reject service of process where a defect in service is found." Combined with the dual email-and-mail requirement and the confirmation-of-receipt rule, a filer that emails only, or that never receives a confirmation email, risks an ineffective service date.
