## Summary

This is the hub page for Covered California eligibility and enrollment. It explains who can apply, what information to gather, four ways to apply (online, by phone, in person, and by mail in 12 languages), and what happens after you enroll. One application checks eligibility for both Covered California and Medi-Cal, and free in-person help from a certified enroller is available at every step.

## Key Facts

- You can apply if you live in California and are a citizen or have a lawful immigration status. Plans are available with or without financial help, depending on your income.
- You do **not** need to submit documents up front; details are checked against government records and information you have already given.
- **Online application:** takes about **30 minutes**, the fastest way, and you can save progress and finish anytime.
- **By phone:** (800) 300-1506 — free local help, multiple languages.
- **In person:** takes about **an hour**, multiple languages, from a certified enroller or licensed insurance agent.
- **By mail:** paper applications available in **12 languages** (Arabic, Armenian, Chinese Traditional, English, Farsi, Hmong, Khmer, Korean, Russian, Spanish, Tagalog, Vietnamese), plus large-print versions.
- Mail completed applications to: **Covered California, P.O. Box 989725, West Sacramento, CA 95798-9725**.
- **Open enrollment:** Nov. 1 to Jan. 31. **Special enrollment:** available year-round.
- Service center: **(800) 300-1506**.

## Details

### Who can apply

You can apply if you live in California and are a citizen or have a lawful immigration status. Plans are available with or without financial help, depending on your income.

### What you need to apply

| What to gather | Who it applies to |
| --- | --- |
| Social Security numbers | Applicants who have them |
| Federal tax information | Applicants who file taxes |
| Immigration documents | Non-citizens |
| Employer and income information | Applicants who earn money |

You do not need to submit documents up front, since your details are checked against government records and information you have already given. If anything further is needed to confirm eligibility, Covered California will contact you with the specific documents to provide.

### Open enrollment vs. special enrollment

| Enrollment type | Timing |
| --- | --- |
| Open enrollment | Nov. 1 to Jan. 31 |
| Special enrollment | Available year-round |

Open enrollment is the time every year where eligible Californians can enroll, renew, or change their plan. Special enrollment is open to Californians who recently experienced a major life change, such as losing health insurance, getting married, having a baby, moving, or changing their immigration status.

### What happens after you enroll

After you enroll and make your first month's payment, you receive a welcome letter and a brochure from Covered California, plus an enrollment package and membership ID card from your health insurance company. Sign in to your account and view your Enrollment Dashboard, or call the service center at (800) 300-1506. If you or a family member are eligible for Medi-Cal, visit BenefitsCal.com or contact your local county office.

### How enrollment works

1. **Apply** — One application covers both Covered California and Medi-Cal.
2. **Choose a plan and enroll** — Compare your options and pick what fits your budget.
3. **Pay your first bill** — Pay online, or wait for a bill from your insurance company; this usually takes about two weeks.
4. **Get your ID card** — Your insurance company mails your membership ID card once they receive your payment. You can use your plan before your ID arrives.

### Common questions

**What if I have a pre-existing condition?**
You cannot be denied health insurance or charged more because of a pre-existing condition. Under the Affordable Care Act every plan has to cover you, even for a condition you had before you enrolled. Coverage for your condition starts as soon as your plan does — there is no waiting period.

**Do I qualify if I'm not a citizen or don't have lawful immigration status?**
No, not for Covered California coverage — but you can still use the application to check if you qualify for Medi-Cal or other programs. Applying will not affect your immigration status or your chances of becoming a permanent resident or citizen. You can apply for your child even if you don't qualify yourself.

**What if I'm not sure which program I qualify for?**
You don't need to know in advance. One application checks your eligibility for both Covered California and Medi-Cal and shows you your options. You don't choose between the two — your application determines which one you qualify for.
