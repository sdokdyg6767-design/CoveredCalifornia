# Major Life Changes

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/major-life-changes
- **Covered California section:** Eligibility & Enrollment — Major Life Changes
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

A major life change — also called a qualifying life event — lets you enroll in a Covered California plan or change your current plan outside open enrollment, through a special-enrollment period. The standard window is 60 days, though losing Medi-Cal gives 90 days and some events permit applying before they happen. The page lists the qualifying events across five categories and gives a separate deadline for each one.

## Key Facts

- The general special-enrollment window is **within 60 days of the event**.
- Losing job-based, student, or other health insurance: **60 days before or after** your coverage ends to apply. Applying before insurance ends avoids a gap.
- Lost Medi-Cal coverage: **within 90 days** of your Medi-Cal ending. Covered California may automatically select a plan for you.
- Moving: **within 60 days** of moving. You may be able to report your move before it happens.
- Released from jail or prison: **within 60 days** of your release.
- Coverage typically starts on the **first of the month after** you choose a plan.
- After release, coverage typically starts on the first of the month after your **release date or plan selection, whichever is later**.
- Pregnancy alone may **not** qualify you for a Covered California plan outside open enrollment — but you can still apply to be screened for Medi-Cal, the Medi-Cal Access Program (MCAP) or another program.
- Having a baby, adopting a child, or beginning foster care **is** a qualifying major life change.

## Details

### What is a major life change?

A major life change — also called a **qualifying life event** — lets you enroll in a Covered California health insurance plan or change your current plan outside the regular open-enrollment period. This is known as a **special-enrollment period**. Not sure if you qualify? You can still apply; Covered California will review your application and let you know what you are eligible for.

### How long you have to act

| Qualifying event | Deadline | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common events (marriage, birth, adoption, divorce, death in household, status changes) | **Within 60 days** of the event | Coverage typically starts the first of the month after you choose a plan |
| Lost health insurance through your job | **60 days before or after** your coverage ends to apply | Apply before insurance ends to avoid a gap |
| Lost Medi-Cal coverage | **Within 90 days** of your Medi-Cal ending | Covered California may automatically select a plan for you |
| Moved to California from another state, or moved within California and plan options changed | **Within 60 days** of moving | You may be able to report your move before it happens; coverage start date may depend on your move date |
| Were released from jail or prison | **Within 60 days** of your release | Coverage typically starts the first of the month after your release date or plan selection, whichever is later |

For some events — such as losing job-based coverage or moving — you may be able to apply **before** the event happens, which can help you avoid a gap in coverage. The application will ask for the date of your life change.

### Qualifying events by category

**Lost or will lose health insurance**

- Lost health insurance through your job
- Lost Medi-Cal coverage
- Lost student health coverage
- COBRA coverage ended
- Aged off a parent's or child-only plan
- Lost insurance after military service
- Lost insurance tied to military service

**Family or household changes**

- Got married or entered a domestic partnership
- Had a baby, adopted a child or began foster care
- Got divorced or legally separated
- Had a death in the household that affects coverage
- Lost insurance after a household member died

**Moved to or within California**

- Moved to California from another state
- Moved within California and plan options changed

**Status changes**

- Gained citizenship, national status or lawful presence

**Special situations**

- Experienced domestic abuse or spousal abandonment
- Were released from jail or prison
- Are a federally recognized American Indian or Alaska Native member

**Changes to existing enrollment**

- Lost financial help after an income change
- Lost financial help after a household change

### COBRA: what counts and what does not

- **COBRA ending because you have exhausted your eligible coverage period** counts as losing coverage and opens a special-enrollment period. You have **60 days before and 60 days after** your coverage ends to apply for a Covered California plan.
- **Stopping your COBRA payments does not count as losing coverage** and does not open a special-enrollment period. To switch to Covered California before COBRA coverage ends, you would need another major life event or wait until open enrollment begins.

### Pregnancy vs. having a baby

Pregnancy alone may not qualify you for a Covered California health insurance plan outside open enrollment. However, you can still apply at any time to see if you qualify for Medi-Cal, the Medi-Cal Access Program (MCAP), or another program. Having a baby, adopting a child, or beginning foster care **is** a qualifying major life change.

### What to do next

**If you are new to Covered California:** shop and compare plans and see if you qualify for financial help before you apply.

**If you already have Covered California:** if your income or household changes, sign in to your account and report the change. Updating your information helps keep your financial help and plan options correct.
