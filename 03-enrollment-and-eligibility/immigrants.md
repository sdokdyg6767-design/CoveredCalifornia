# Information for Immigrants

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/eligibility/immigrants
- **Covered California section:** Eligibility & Enrollment — Specific Populations — Immigrants
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains how immigration status affects Covered California and Medi-Cal eligibility. Anyone can apply, and applying will not affect immigration status or the chance of becoming a lawful permanent resident or U.S. citizen, but only lawfully present people qualify for Covered California plans. Medi-Cal rules differ by age, and a January 2026 state change ended full Medi-Cal enrollment for adults without a qualifying immigration status. It also covers the 2027 federal change that limits premium tax credit eligibility to green card holders and two other groups.

## Key Facts

- **Anyone can apply.** Applying will not affect your immigration status or your chances of becoming a lawful permanent resident or U.S. citizen.
- List **everyone** in your household on the application, regardless of immigration status or whether they are applying for insurance.
- **People not lawfully present are not eligible for Covered California**, but may qualify for Medi-Cal or another program.
- **Children ages 0-18:** eligible for full Medi-Cal coverage regardless of immigration status.
- **Pregnant people:** eligible for full Medi-Cal during pregnancy and for **up to one year after** the pregnancy ends, regardless of immigration status.
- **Adults ages 19 and older:** starting **Jan. 1, 2026**, adults without a qualifying immigration status can no longer enroll in full Medi-Cal. Those already enrolled before that date can keep coverage as long as they remain eligible and renew during their renewal month. *(Verified verbatim against the live page on 2026-10-13: "Starting on Jan. 1, 2026, adults without a qualifying immigration status can no longer enroll in full Medi-Cal.")*
- **Emergency care:** everyone can access emergency Medi-Cal for serious medical emergencies, and emergency dental care for severe pain, infection, or tooth removal.
- **DACA:** as of **August 2025**, DACA recipients are no longer eligible to enroll through Covered California. They are still eligible for Medi-Cal if they meet income and household size requirements.
- **You can apply without a Social Security number.** If you have one, you should include it, but it is not required.
- Service center: **(800) 300-1506**. Health Consumer Alliance: **(888) 804-3536**.

### Financial help in 2027 (federal change)

President Trump signed **H.R. 1**, also known as the **One Big Beautiful Bill Act**, which makes certain immigrant groups with legal status ineligible for financial help.

Only the following immigrant groups will be eligible for financial help to lower the cost of their health insurance in **2027**:

- Lawful permanent residents (green card holders)
- Cuban and Haitian entrants
- Compact of Free Association (COFA) migrants

Major immigrant groups that will **no longer** have financial help:

- People granted asylum status
- Refugees
- Survivors of trafficking (T visa)
- People with Temporary Protected Status
- People with work visas
- Student visa holders

Your costs in **2026 do not change**. Some immigrants and their families will have higher monthly premium costs in **2027**, but can still keep health insurance through Covered California.

### What can I do now?

1. **Review your information.** Make sure your information is up to date in your account. Update your application if your immigration status changed.
2. **Keep using your current health plan.** Your current health plan costs are not changing in 2026, and you will continue to get financial help in 2026.
3. **Shop for a new plan during the renewal period.** Compare plan options and costs using the Shop and Compare Tool.

See the **Lawfully Present Immigrant (LPI) Consumer Fact Sheet** for more information about changes starting in 2027.

### Do not confuse three separate dates

These are easy to conflate and are frequently reported incorrectly:

| Date | What actually happens | Source |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Jan 1, 2026** | **State change.** Adults 19+ without a qualifying immigration status can no longer enroll in full Medi-Cal. Existing enrollees keep coverage if they remain eligible and renew in their renewal month. | coveredca.com immigrants page (verified) |
| **September 2026** | **Federal public-charge rule** finalized; changes how public-charge risk is determined. Can affect green card applications for people using financial help. | coveredca.com immigrants page (verified) |
| **2027 plan year** | **Federal premium-tax-credit restriction.** Higher premiums for some immigrant families; APTC limited to green card holders and two other groups. Costs in 2026 do not change. | coveredca.com important-changes (verified) |

> **Note for researchers.** The separate federal *community engagement / work requirement* in P.L. 119-21 (SEC. 71119) applies "beginning not later than the first day of the first quarter that begins after December 31, 2026" — i.e. **April 1, 2027**, with states permitted to start earlier via a §1115 waiver. That federal Medicaid work-requirement change is a **different provision** from the California Jan 1, 2026 coverage ending above. Do not use April 2027 to contradict the January 2026 state date, and vice versa. California DHCS was unreachable from this environment, so California's own implementation date for the federal work requirement is **not verified here**. See `../../09-external-databases/medi-cal-administration-sources.md`.

### Mixed-status families

A mixed-status family has members with different immigration statuses. List all family members on the application regardless of status or whether they are applying for insurance. Household size and income — including information about all members — are used to calculate financial help and determine eligibility for everyone in the family. Each person's eligibility is considered separately.

### Public charge and your immigration status

"Public charge" describes someone whom the government determines is likely to depend heavily on certain public benefits in the future. The federal government finalized a **new rule beginning in September 2026** that changes how it determines public charge risk. Depending on your situation and immigration status, getting financial help through Covered California may affect your green card application. Most consumers who are eligible for financial help in **2027** may not be subject to the public charge rule when they apply for admission or a green card.

The public-charge rule does **not** apply to green card applicants getting a green card based on:

- Asylum
- Refugee status
- U visa
- T visa
- Violence Against Women Act (VAWA)
- Special Immigrant Juvenile Status

Covered California advises consulting an attorney or qualified immigration legal representative before making decisions.

### Privacy concerns

If you are lawfully present, a green card holder, or a U.S. citizen, Covered California protects your information and it will not be shared with immigration authorities. If you are undocumented, note that Covered California and Medi-Cal use the same application, and a **December 2025 court ruling** allows federal authorities to access certain basic information (like name, address, date of birth, and phone number) for Medi-Cal members who are not lawfully residing in the United States. California has not yet received guidance on how or whether this will be carried out. See DHCS's statement on Medi-Cal data and member privacy.

### Where to get help

- Covered California service center: **(800) 300-1506**, or find a certified enroller who speaks your language.
- Health Consumer Alliance: **(888) 804-3536** — free legal help with health insurance, serves all Californians, in all languages, at no cost.
- California Department of Social Services — a list of community organizations providing free, confidential legal help related to public charge.
