## Summary

This page helps consumers with ongoing health needs compare plans beyond the monthly premium, focusing on network, formulary, and pharmacy fit. It explains continuity of care — the temporary continuation of treatment after switching plans — and notes it is not automatic and requires the doctor or hospital to agree. It also covers the grievance process for denied or delayed requests, and the AIDS Drug Assistance Program (ADAP) and Ryan White Program resources available to people living with HIV or AIDS.

## Key Facts

- **Continuity of care** is not automatic; your doctor or hospital must agree to the arrangement.
- Continuity of care decisions are made between you, your health insurance company, and your doctor or hospital. Covered California is not involved in these decisions.
- Not all conditions or situations qualify for continuity of care.
- Health Consumer Alliance: **(888) 804-3536** — free, confidential, local help with the grievance process.
- California Department of Managed Health Care Help Center: **(888) 466-2219**.
- ADAP (AIDS Drug Assistance Program), California Department of Public Health: **(844) 421-7050**.
- California Department of Public Health Office of AIDS: **(916) 449-5900**.

## Details

### What to check before you choose a plan

When you have ongoing health needs, comparing plans means looking beyond the monthly **premium** (the amount you pay each month for your health insurance plan). Before choosing a plan, check whether:

| Check | What it means |
| --- | --- |
| Network | Your doctors and hospitals are in the plan's network |
| Formulary | Your medications are on the plan's drug list (called a formulary) |
| Pharmacy of choice | Your pharmacy of choice works with the plan |
| Continuity of treatment | Scheduled treatments, tests, therapy or other care will continue with your new plan |

### Premium vs. total cost

Plans with lower monthly premiums often have higher **deductibles** (the amount you pay out of pocket before your health insurance company starts paying for some covered services) and **copays** (a set amount you pay for covered services, like a doctor visit), which can add up quickly if you use care often.

### Changing plans while getting treatment

Some people being treated for a serious condition can temporarily continue care with their doctor, hospital, or other provider after switching plans or changing networks. This is called **continuity of care**. Key points:

- It is **not automatic** — your doctor or hospital must agree to the arrangement.
- **Not all conditions or situations qualify.**
- Contact your new health insurance company about your situation and how to request it.
- Tell your current doctor that you have new health insurance.
- Covered California is not involved in these decisions.

In a limited cost-sharing plan context, if you have a referral from an Indian health care provider you do not pay out of pocket for outside care; without a referral, out-of-pocket costs are based on the coverage level chosen.

### Denied or delayed continuity of care requests

If your continuity of care request is denied or delayed, you can file a formal grievance (also known as a complaint or appeal) with your health insurance company. Ask them how to start that process.

For free, confidential, local help with the grievance process, contact the **Health Consumer Alliance at (888) 804-3536**. They can help you work with your health insurance company and, if necessary, with health insurance regulators. They can also help you dispute denials of health services, prevent disruption in care, and resolve billing problems.

You can also contact the **California Department of Managed Health Care's Help Center at (888) 466-2219** if:

- You need medications or care urgently and your health insurance company is not responding.
- Your grievance is not solved to your satisfaction.

### Support for people living with HIV or AIDS

**Help paying for HIV prescription drugs and premiums.** The **AIDS Drug Assistance Program (ADAP)** within the California Department of Public Health helps eligible people who are uninsured or underinsured access HIV prescription drugs. For ADAP eligibility information, visit the Office of AIDS ADAP Eligibility webpage or call **(844) 421-7050**.

If you are already enrolled in ADAP, you may also get help with:

| Assistance | Condition |
| --- | --- |
| Prescription drug deductibles and copays for drugs on the ADAP formulary | Your pharmacy must also participate in ADAP |
| Paying your Covered California monthly premiums | Through the Office of AIDS's Health Insurance Premium Payment (OA-HIPP) program |

**Additional services and resources.** People living with HIV or AIDS may be eligible for additional services funded through the **Ryan White Program**, including case management, nutritional support, transportation, and free legal services. Contact the California Department of Public Health's Office of AIDS at **(916) 449-5900** for more information.
