# Minimum Essential Coverage

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/documents/minimum-essential-coverage
- **Covered California section:** Eligibility & Enrollment — Documents — Proof You Don't Have Minimum Essential Coverage
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains how to prove you do not have minimum essential coverage, which is required in California to avoid the state tax penalty for not having insurance. It lists the accepted documents, the self-attestation alternative, and a dedicated set of rules and forms for proving claims about Medicare eligibility or enrollment.

## Key Facts

- Minimum essential coverage is required in California to avoid a **state tax penalty** for not having insurance.
- An employer plan counts as minimum essential coverage only if it offers an affordable option paying for **at least 60% of covered medical costs** (minimum value).
- Qualifying types include job-based health plans, government-sponsored programs like **Medicare or Medi-Cal**, individual plans offered through private insurance companies, and student health plans.
- If you cannot provide a listed document, you may submit a signed **self-attestation** under penalty of perjury using the Attestation of Lack of Minimum Coverage form (PDF).
- A Social Security document showing you do not pay a premium for "Medical Insurance" refers to **Medicare Part B — not Part A**, and cannot verify Part A eligibility.
- Medicare-related proof may show coverage ending or will end **within the next 90 days** for people no longer eligible for SSDI benefits.

## Details

### Before you submit

Minimum essential coverage is required in California to avoid a **state tax penalty** for not having insurance.

| Type of minimum essential coverage | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| Health plans through your job | Must offer an **affordable option that pays for at least 60% of covered medical costs** |
| Government-sponsored programs | Such as Medicare or Medi-Cal |
| Individual plans | Offered through private insurance companies |
| Student health plans | — |

Covered California compares the information on your application with records from government sources, such as the IRS and Social Security Administration. If the information does not match, you may be asked to confirm that you do not have qualifying health insurance.

### Documents you can submit

Submit a copy of **one** of the following documents.

| Document | What it shows |
| --- | --- |
| Certificate of coverage, or a letter from a health insurance company | When your insurance ended |
| Government-issued document | When your health insurance ended, or that you are not eligible for a program |
| Employer statement | That your job does not offer affordable, minimum-value health insurance |
| Signed self-attestation | A written statement confirming you do not have qualifying health insurance, signed under penalty of perjury — use the **Attestation of Lack of Minimum Coverage form (PDF)** |

### If your proof is about Medicare

To verify Medicare eligibility or enrollment status, submit a letter or statement from Medicare or the Social Security Administration showing that you or your family member is:

| Situation | Accepted statement |
| --- | --- |
| Not eligible for or enrolled in premium-free Medicare Part A | Medicare or SSA letter or statement |
| Eligible for, but not enrolled in, Part A coverage that requires monthly premium payments | Medicare or SSA letter or statement |
| No longer eligible for Social Security Disability Insurance (SSDI) benefits | Your coverage has ended or will end **within the next 90 days** |

**Note:** A Social Security document showing you do not pay a premium for "Medical Insurance" refers to **Medicare Part B — not Part A**. This document **cannot be used** to verify Medicare Part A eligibility.

If you cannot get a letter from Medicare or the Social Security Administration, download the **Attestation of Medicare Eligibility and Enrollment Status (PDF)** form. Once complete, submit it by uploading it to your online account, or by fax or mail using the contact information on the form.

### How to submit

Follow the instructions in your Covered California account or eligibility notice to submit your document online, by fax, or by mail.
