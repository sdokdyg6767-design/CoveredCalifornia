# Domestic Abuse or Spousal Abandonment

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/major-life-changes/domestic-abuse
- **Covered California section:** Eligibility & Enrollment — Major Life Changes — Domestic Abuse
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Domestic abuse or spousal abandonment is a qualifying major life change, giving you a special-enrollment period to apply for health insurance outside open enrollment. You generally have 60 days to enroll and do not need to provide proof of the abuse. The page explains how to answer the marital status and tax filing questions so you can apply without entering your spouse's information, and how to remove a partner from an existing Covered California or Medi-Cal account.

## Key Facts

- Domestic abuse or spousal abandonment qualifies you for a **special-enrollment period**.
- If you qualify, you generally have **60 days** to enroll in a health plan.
- **You don't need to provide proof.** Your experience is enough to get started.
- You do **not** need to currently have health insurance through Covered California to qualify.
- Your children or other dependents may also qualify.
- Enter **"single"** as marital status even if you are still married — this lets you apply without entering your spouse's information.
- Tax filing status: enter **"head of household"** if you claim a dependent child (natural child, stepchild, adopted child or foster child) on your taxes. Enter **"single"** if you don't claim dependents.
- Do **not** enter "married filing jointly" or "married filing separately," and do **not** enter your spouse's income.
- Covered California phone support: **(800) 300-1506**, Monday through Friday, **8 a.m. to 6 p.m.**

## Details

### What to know

- Domestic abuse or spousal abandonment qualifies you for a **special-enrollment period**, meaning you can apply for health insurance outside the regular open-enrollment period.
- If you qualify, you generally have **60 days** to enroll in a health plan.
- You do not need to provide proof; your experience is enough to get started.
- You do not need to currently have health insurance through Covered California to qualify.
- Your children or other dependents may also qualify.

### If you're applying for new insurance

When you apply, you are asked about your marital status and tax filing status. Answer as follows:

| Application question | How to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Marital status | Enter **"single"** even if you are still married. This lets you apply without entering your spouse's information. |
| Tax filing status — you claim a dependent child | Enter **"head of household"**. A dependent child may be a natural child, stepchild, adopted child or foster child. |
| Tax filing status — you do not claim dependents | Enter **"single"** |

**Do not:**

- Enter "married filing jointly" or "married filing separately."
- Enter your spouse's income.

### If you already have health insurance

**Covered California:** to remove a partner or dependent from your account or set up separate health insurance, call **(800) 300-1506**, Monday through Friday, **8 a.m. to 6 p.m.** Free in-person help from a certified enroller is also available through the site's ways to get help.

**Medi-Cal:** if you or your household members have Medi-Cal, contact your **county Medi-Cal office** to remove a partner from your account or set up separate insurance.

### Common questions

**Do I need to submit forms or documents?**
No. Select **"domestic abuse or spousal abandonment"** as your major life change when you apply. You do not need to provide any forms or documents.

**What if I'm still married?**
You can still apply. Enter **"single"** as your marital status when you apply to avoid entering your spouse's information. You will not be penalized for this.

**How do I get help removing a partner from my account?**
Contact Covered California at (800) 300-1506 for help removing a partner or dependent from your account or setting up separate insurance. If you have Medi-Cal, contact your county Medi-Cal office.

### Tax considerations

If you receive federal tax credits or state premium assistance, you may need to complete additional tax forms when you file your federal and state tax returns. California and federal law have special tax filing rules for survivors of domestic abuse and spousal abandonment. Covered California cannot give tax advice — contact a tax professional or visit the IRS website with questions about your tax situation.
