# Losing Medi-Cal Coverage

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/losing-medi-cal-coverage
- **Covered California section:** Eligibility & Enrollment — Loss of Medi-Cal Coverage
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

If you lose Medi-Cal eligibility, California law requires Covered California to enroll you in a new plan so you do not have a gap in coverage. If you qualify for financial help, Covered California picks the plan offering you the most financial help — but you do not have to keep it. You have one month from the date your Medi-Cal ends to select a plan without a coverage gap, though you can still enroll for up to 90 days.

## Key Facts

- You have **one month** from the date your Medi-Cal ends to select a plan without a coverage gap.
- You can still enroll for **up to 90 days**, but waiting may leave you without insurance.
- If you qualify for financial help, Covered California selected the plan that offered you the **most financial help** — you do not have to keep it.
- Your notice includes your **access code, case number and deadline**, and whether a plan has already been selected for you.
- Medi-Cal eligibility is based on your **household size and income**; a change in either can end eligibility.
- Find your local Medi-Cal office on **DHCS.ca.gov** if you think you received the notice by mistake.
- Service center: **(800) 300-1506**.

## Details

### Your Medi-Cal is ending

You received the notice because you or someone in your household is no longer eligible for Medi-Cal. California law requires Covered California to enroll you in a new plan.

- Whether a plan was selected for you depends on **whether you qualify for financial help**.
- If you do qualify, Covered California picked the plan that offered you the **most financial help** — but you don't have to keep it.
- Your notice includes your **access code, case number and deadline**, and tells you whether a plan has already been selected and how much financial help you may qualify for.
- You have **one month** from the date your Medi-Cal ends to select a plan without a coverage gap. You can still enroll for **up to 90 days**, but waiting may leave you without insurance.
- Sign in to your account to keep, change, or cancel your plan.

### Why Medi-Cal eligibility can change

Medi-Cal eligibility is based on your **household size and income**. If either of these changed, you may no longer qualify. You can get health insurance through Covered California — usually with financial help to lower your monthly costs. Medi-Cal and Covered California are both part of the same system, and moving between them is normal.

### What to do next

| Step | What to do |
| --- | --- |
| 1. Check your notice | Look for your access code, case number and deadline. Even if a new plan was picked for you, you don't have to keep it. |
| 2. Review your options | Compare plans and costs before you decide. |
| 3. Keep, cancel or change your plan | Sign in to your account to keep, change or cancel it. If your new plan includes a monthly premium, make your first payment to avoid a gap in coverage. |

### What situation are you in?

| Your situation | What applies |
| --- | --- |
| California picked a plan for you | California law requires Covered California to enroll you so you don't have a gap. Covered California picked the one with the most financial help. You don't have to keep the plan chosen for you. |
| You may not qualify for financial help | Double-check the information on your application and see if you qualify. If you still don't qualify, compare plans and costs, then select a plan and make your first payment. |
| Your county may need to review or correct your information | Contact your local Medi-Cal county office. |
| Your doctor isn't in the new network | You can keep your doctor if they are in your new plan's network. If not, choose a different plan or change doctors. |
| You want to know what your new plan covers | Your new plan includes many of the same benefits you have now, including free preventive care and screenings. |
| Your employer offers insurance | Depending on the insurance your employer offers, you might not qualify for a Covered California plan. Sign in or create an account to review your options. |

### What is financial help?

Financial help — also called **premium tax credits** — lowers your monthly premium (what you pay to your health insurance company each month) and your out-of-pocket costs (health care costs you pay yourself, like deductibles, copays, and coinsurance). Your monthly cost depends on the financial help you qualify for and the plan you choose; the financial help available to you is based on your **income and household size**.

### If you think the notice is a mistake

If you think you received the notice by mistake, find your local Medi-Cal office on **DHCS.ca.gov**.
