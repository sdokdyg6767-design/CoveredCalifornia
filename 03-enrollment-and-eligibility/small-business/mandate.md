# Employer Mandate — Covered California for Small Business

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/small-business/mandate
- **Covered California section:** For Small Business — Employer Mandate
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Under the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, businesses with **50 or more full-time-equivalent employees** are generally considered applicable large employers and may be required to offer qualifying health insurance or face the employer shared responsibility payment. This page sets out the standards an applicable large employer must meet, exactly how the penalty is calculated, and the 1095-C reporting obligations. Critically, the IRS — not Covered California — determines whether a penalty is owed.

## Key Facts

- Applicable large employers are businesses with **50 or more full-time-equivalent employees**.
- Qualifying insurance must be offered to **at least 95 percent of full-time employees** and their dependents.
- **Minimum value:** the plan must pay for **at least 60 percent** of the total average cost of covered services.
- **Affordability:** the employee's premium must **not exceed 9.96 percent of their household income**.
- If insurance is not offered to at least 95 percent of full-time employees, the monthly payment equals the number of full-time-equivalent employees **(minus the first 30)** multiplied by **one-twelfth of $2,000**.
- If insurance fails minimum value or affordability, the monthly payment is the **lesser of** one-twelfth of **$3,000** per full-time employee receiving premium tax credits, or one-twelfth of **$2,000** per full-time employee (minus the first 30).
- Applicable large employers must file **IRS Form 1095-C** for each employee and a **Form 1094-C Transmittal**.
- **All eligible employees should receive a Form 1095-C**, regardless of whether they enrolled in the employer's plan.
- Employers with fewer than 50 full-time employees are generally not subject to these reporting requirements; their employees may receive a **Form 1095-B** from the health insurance company instead.
- If you get an appeal notice from Covered California, you generally have **90 days** from the date of the notice to request an appeal. **Employer appeals are handled by the IRS.**

## Details

### What is the employer mandate?

Under the Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act, businesses with **50 or more full-time-equivalent employees** are generally considered **applicable large employers**. Applicable large employers may be required to offer health insurance meeting certain standards to their full-time employees and their dependents, or potentially face a financial penalty called the **employer shared responsibility payment** — a per-employee, per-month fee.

**The IRS makes employer shared responsibility determinations.** Covered California for Small Business can help eligible employers offer qualifying insurance, but **it does not determine whether your business owes a penalty**. For detailed guidance, see IRS Employer Shared Responsibility Provisions.

### Does the mandate apply to your business?

Whether the mandate applies depends on your **full-time-equivalent employee count**. A full-time-equivalent employee is not simply a headcount — it is a calculation that combines full-time and part-time employee hours.

| FTE count | Result |
| --- | --- |
| **Fewer than 50 full-time employees** | Generally **no** — you are not subject to employer shared responsibility provisions. If you choose to offer insurance, it must still meet Affordable Care Act requirements. |
| **50 or more full-time employees** | Likely **yes** — you may be an applicable large employer and required to offer qualifying health insurance or face IRS fees. |

### How to meet health insurance requirements

If your business is an applicable large employer, the health insurance plan you offer generally needs to meet a few standards:

| Standard | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Coverage** | Offered to **at least 95 percent of full-time employees** and their dependents |
| **ACA requirements** | Coverage of **essential health benefits** and **no lifetime or annual benefit limits** |
| **Minimum value** | The plan pays for **at least 60 percent** of the total average cost of covered services |
| **Affordability** | The employee's premium does **not exceed 9.96 percent of their household income** |

All health insurance plans offered through CCSB provide **minimum essential coverage**. If a business offers insurance for employees' dependents, those dependents are **ineligible for financial help** with a Covered California health insurance plan.

### When the employer shared responsibility payment applies

The payment is only triggered when **at least one full-time employee receives a premium tax credit** to buy health insurance through a marketplace like Covered California — which happens when your business does not meet one or more of the standards above.

You do **not** owe a payment simply because employees purchase insurance through Covered California or have Medicare or Medi-Cal.

If the IRS determines your business may owe a payment, **it will contact you directly before assessing any liability** and will give you an opportunity to respond.

### How the payment is calculated

| Situation | Calculation |
| --- | --- |
| **Does not offer insurance to at least 95% of full-time employees** | Number of full-time-equivalent employees **(minus the first 30)** multiplied by **one-twelfth of $2,000**, assessed monthly |
| **Offers insurance that fails minimum value or affordability** | The **lesser of**: one-twelfth of **$3,000** per full-time employee receiving premium tax credits, **or** one-twelfth of **$2,000** per full-time employee (minus the first 30) |

### Reporting forms and employer notices

Applicable large employers (those with **50 or more full-time employees**) are required to complete **IRS Form 1095-C** for each employee and submit a **Form 1094-C Transmittal** to the IRS. These forms report the health insurance offered to employees for the prior year, including the lowest-cost premiums available and months of the year health insurance was offered.

**All eligible employees should receive a Form 1095-C, regardless of whether they enrolled in the employer's health insurance plan.**

Employers with fewer than 50 full-time employees are generally not subject to these reporting requirements; their employees may receive a **Form 1095-B** from the health insurance company instead.

### Employer notices and response

If the IRS determines your business may owe an employer shared responsibility payment, it will contact you directly before any liability is assessed. Employers may have the right to respond or appeal. If you get a notice from Covered California, you generally have **90 days from the date of that notice** to request an appeal. **Employer appeals are handled by the IRS.**

### Common questions

**Do I have to offer insurance if I have fewer than 50 full-time employees?**
No. Businesses with fewer than 50 full-time employees, including full-time equivalents, are generally **not required** to offer health insurance under the Affordable Care Act. However, if you choose to offer insurance, it must comply with Affordable Care Act requirements, including insurance of the **10 essential health benefits** and **no lifetime or annual benefit maximums**.

**What is a full-time-equivalent employee?**
A full-time-equivalent employee is not simply a headcount. It is a calculation that combines the hours of part-time and full-time employees to determine whether your business meets the applicable large employer threshold. Tools to calculate your FTE count are available under mandate resources.

**Who assesses the penalty?**
The IRS administers employer shared responsibility determinations and will contact your business directly if it believes a payment may be owed. Covered California for Small Business helps eligible employers offer qualifying insurance, but **it does not assess penalties**.
