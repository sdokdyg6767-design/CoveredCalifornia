# Financial Help for Employers

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/small-business/costs-and-financial-help
- **Covered California section:** For Small Business — Costs and Financial Help
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Financial help for small businesses through Covered California for Small Business is a **federal tax credit**, not the premium assistance available to individuals. The credit is worth up to 50 percent of premium contributions for for-profit employers and up to 35 percent for tax-exempt employers, applies for up to two consecutive taxable years, and is determined on a sliding scale by employee count, average wages, and employer contribution. CCSB does not determine tax eligibility — the IRS does.

## Key Facts

- For-profit employers: credit of **up to 50 percent of eligible premium expenses**.
- Tax-exempt employers: credit of **up to 35 percent of eligible premium expenses**.
- The credit applies for **up to two consecutive taxable years**. After that, the business is no longer eligible even if it continues to offer insurance through CCSB.
- Your business must have **fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees** for the credit.
- Owners and immediate family members of the owner are **not counted** when calculating the credit.
- Average annual wages must be **below the IRS threshold**, adjusted for inflation each year — check IRS Form 8941 for the current figure.
- You must contribute **at least 50 percent** of the cost of health insurance for each employee.
- **CCSB does not determine your tax eligibility** — the IRS does.
- Federal tax credit income limits are adjusted annually, typically in the **first quarter of the following calendar year**.
- CCSB phone: **(844) 332-8384** for new customers, **(855) 777-6782** for existing customers, Monday through Friday, **8 a.m. to 5 p.m.**

## Details

### Financial help for small businesses is different

Eligible small businesses may be able to get a **federal tax credit available only through CCSB**. The amount is based on factors like business size, average employee wages, and how much the employer contributes toward premiums. This is **not** the same as the premium assistance available to individuals and families buying insurance through Covered California — the two programs have different rules, thresholds, and calculations.

### What the tax credit helps pay for

The federal small business health care tax credit can reduce the overall cost of providing health insurance to your employees. Eligible small businesses can receive **up to 50 percent of their premium contributions back**.

### Maximum credit amounts

| Organization type | Maximum credit |
| --- | --- |
| **For-profit employers** | Up to **50 percent** of eligible premium expenses |
| **Tax-exempt employers** | Up to **35 percent** of eligible premium expenses |

### How long the credit applies

The credit applies for **up to two consecutive taxable years**. After that period, your business is **no longer eligible** for the credit, even if you continue to offer insurance through CCSB.

### What affects your tax credit

The credit is based on a **sliding scale**. Smaller businesses with lower average employee wages may qualify for a larger credit.

| Factor | Requirement |
| --- | --- |
| **Number of employees** | Your business must have **fewer than 25 full-time equivalent employees**. Owners and immediate family members of the owner are **not counted**. |
| **Average annual wages** | Your employees must have an average annual wage **below the IRS threshold**. This limit is adjusted for inflation each year — check IRS Form 8941 for the most current figure. |
| **Employer monthly premium contribution** | You must contribute **at least 50 percent** of the cost of health insurance for each employee. |

A full-time-equivalent (FTE) employee is a calculation that combines the hours of all part-time and full-time employees. For tax-credit purposes, your business must have **fewer than 25 FTEs**. Owners and immediate family members of the owner are not counted.

### How employer and employee costs work

When you offer insurance through CCSB, **you choose how much to contribute** toward your employees' premiums. Your employees pay the remaining portion, which depends on the plan they choose.

The cost of insurance for your business depends on:

- The **size and location** of your business
- The **ages of your employees**
- The **health and dental plans** you select
- The **metal tier level** you set as your reference plan

Employers can control their costs by setting a **contribution level tied to a reference plan**. Employees can then choose from available plans within the coverage levels you offer.

### Before you claim the credit

Not all businesses qualify. **CCSB does not determine your tax eligibility — that is determined by the IRS** based on your specific situation. To verify whether your business qualifies:

- **Consult a professional tax adviser.** A licensed tax professional can review your business details and confirm eligibility.
- **Review IRS guidance,** including the Small Business Health Care Tax Credit and **IRS Form 8941: Credit for Small Employer Health Insurance Premiums**.
- **Check annually.** Federal tax credit income limits are adjusted each year as updated IRS publications become available, typically in the **first quarter of the following calendar year**.

### Common questions

**Do all CCSB businesses get the credit?** No. The credit applies only to businesses that meet specific IRS requirements, including employee count, average wages, and employer contribution levels. Not all businesses that offer insurance through CCSB will qualify. Consult a professional tax adviser.

**Is this the same as the help available to individuals?** No. The financial help on this page is a **federal tax credit for small-business employers** — it is not the same as the premium assistance available to individuals and families who buy insurance through Covered California.
