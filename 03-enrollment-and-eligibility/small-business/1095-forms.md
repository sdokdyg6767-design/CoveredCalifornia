# 1095 Forms and Reporting — Covered California for Small Business

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/small-business/mandate/1095-forms
- **Covered California section:** For Small Business — Employer Mandate — 1095 Forms and Reporting
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains which IRS 1095 reporting forms apply to a CCSB business. Employers with fewer than 50 full-time employees generally rely on Form 1095-B issued by the health insurance company or government agency, while applicable large employers must file Form 1095-C for every full-time employee plus a Form 1094-C transmittal. It also covers W-2 Box 12 Code DD reporting, Additional Medicare Tax, and the rule that Covered California itself does not issue or correct any 1095 form.

## Key Facts

- Small employers (fewer than 50 full-time employees, including FTEs) are **not subject to the employer shared responsibility provisions**, but may still have reporting responsibilities.
- **You do not need to file Form 1095-B with the IRS** as a small employer using a fully insured plan.
- Applicable large employers must issue **Form 1095-C to each full-time employee** for one or more months of the calendar year — **including employees who did not enroll**.
- Employers must also send an **annual statement to all eligible employees** describing the health and dental insurance available to them.
- Small employers must report the cost of applicable employer-sponsored insurance on each employee's **Form W-2, Box 12, Code DD**. This is **informational only** and is not taxable income. The amount should include both the employer-paid and employee-paid portions.
- **Additional Medicare Tax:** employers must withhold and report an additional **0.9 percent** of Medicare Tax on employee wages or compensation that exceed **$200,000**.
- Employers are **not required** to issue a Form W-2 solely to report health insurance costs for retirees or former employees who would not otherwise receive a W-2.
- If an employee received a **Form 1095-A**, they were enrolled in an **individual-market** Covered California plan, not a CCSB plan.
- Medi-Cal 1095-B Help Desk: **(844) 253-0883**; TTY: **(844) 357-5709**.

## Details

### What Forms 1095-B and 1095-C report

The Affordable Care Act requires employers that provide **applicable employer-sponsored health insurance** to report that insurance to the IRS and to employees. Forms 1095-B and 1095-C are the IRS statements used to fulfill this requirement.

| Form | Who files it | Who it covers | What it reports |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1095-B** | The **health insurance company** (for employers with fewer than 50 full-time-equivalent employees) or a **government agency** (for Medi-Cal and other government-sponsored insurance) | Employees enrolled in a Covered California for Small Business health insurance plan, or recipients of government-sponsored insurance | **Months of minimum essential coverage** |
| **1095-C** | The **employer** (for applicable large employers with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees) | Full-time employees of applicable large employers | **Months of employer-sponsored insurance offered** and **employee premium share** |
| **W-2 Box 12, Code DD** | The **employer** | All employees receiving applicable employer-sponsored insurance | **Cost of employer-sponsored health insurance** (informational only; not taxable income) |

### Businesses with fewer than 50 full-time employees

Small employers, generally those with fewer than 50 full-time employees including full-time equivalents, are **not subject to the employer shared responsibility provisions**. However, you may still have reporting responsibilities.

**Form 1095-B.** If your employees are enrolled in a Covered California for Small Business health insurance plan, the **health insurance company will generally issue Form 1095-B** to each enrolled employee. If your employees are covered by Medi-Cal or another government-sponsored program, the **government agency** will issue Form 1095-B instead.

You **do not need to file Form 1095-B with the IRS** as a small employer using a fully insured plan.

**W-2 reporting.** Small employers that provide applicable employer-sponsored insurance under a group health insurance plan are required to report the cost of that insurance on each employee's **Form W-2 in Box 12, Code DD**. This reporting is for **informational purposes only** — it does not mean the insurance is taxable income for the employee. The amount reported should include **both the portion paid by the employer and the portion paid by the employee**. Coverage types vary in whether they must be reported, are optional, or are not required.

> **Note:** Employers are **not required** to issue a Form W-2 solely to report health insurance costs for retirees or former employees who would not otherwise receive a W-2.

**Additional Medicare Tax.** Additional Medicare Tax applies to wages, compensation, and self-employment income above a threshold amount. Employers must withhold and report an additional **0.9 percent** of Medicare Tax on employee wages or compensation that exceed **$200,000**.

### Special small-employer situations

| Situation | Consequence |
| --- | --- |
| **Self-insured plans** | If your business sponsors a self-insured health insurance plan, you may have additional IRS reporting responsibilities **even if you have fewer than 50 full-time employees**. Consult a tax professional. |
| **Common ownership** | If your business has fewer than 50 full-time employees but is part of an ownership group with **50 or more full-time equivalent employees combined**, you may be subject to the rules for applicable large employers. |

### Businesses that are applicable large employers

An applicable large employer is generally a business with **50 or more full-time employees**, including full-time-equivalent employees, during the prior calendar year.

**Form 1095-C.** As an applicable large employer, you are required to issue **Form 1095-C to each full-time employee** for one or more months of the calendar year — **including employees who did not enroll** in your health insurance plan. You must also send an **annual statement to all eligible employees** describing the health and dental insurance available to them. All Covered California for Small Business health insurance plans provide **minimum essential coverage**.

**Form 1094-C transmittal.** Form 1094-C is the transmittal form you file with the IRS along with your Forms 1095-C. It summarizes:

- The total number of forms issued
- Employee counts by month
- Whether you offered coverage to **at least 95 percent** of your full-time employees and their dependents

### Information you may need for Form 1095-C and 1094-C

If your business is an applicable large employer, gather the following before completing your annual reporting forms:

- Identifying information for your business and each full-time employee, **including names and addresses**
- Whether insurance was offered to **at least 95%** of full-time employees and their dependents
- Information about any **aggregated applicable large employer group** members
- **Full-time and total employee counts by month**
- **Total number of Forms 1095-C issued**
- Whether you are eligible for certain **transition relief**

### How employees use Forms 1095-B and 1095-C

Employees who receive Form 1095-B or Form 1095-C should keep these forms with their tax records. These forms show proof of health insurance and may be used when filing a tax return.

- Employees **do not need to wait** for Form 1095-B or 1095-C to file their taxes.
- Employees **should not attach** these forms to their tax return.
- If an employee has questions about information on their form, they should contact **the party that issued it** — the health insurance company (for Form 1095-B) or the employer (for Form 1095-C).

**Covered California does not issue, correct, or resolve disputes for Forms 1095-B or 1095-C.**

| Form an employee received | Meaning | Who to contact |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1095-A** | Enrolled in an **individual-market** Covered California plan, not a CCSB plan | The individual-market issuer |
| **1095-B** | Covered by an individual-market plan or a government program | The health insurance company or government agency |
| **1095-C** | Employer's report | **Your employer**, or the contact information provided with the form |

Covered California does not correct Forms 1095-B issued by health insurance companies or government programs. **Medi-Cal recipients who received a Form 1095-B** and need assistance should contact their **county Medi-Cal office** or call the **Medi-Cal 1095-B Help Desk at (844) 253-0883 (TTY: (844) 357-5709)**.

Self-insured employers — including small employers with fewer than 50 full-time employees — may have additional IRS reporting responsibilities. If your business has fewer than 50 full-time employees but belongs to an ownership group with 50 or more full-time equivalent employees combined, you may be treated as an applicable large employer for reporting purposes.

### Finding a tax professional

The **IRS Directory of Federal Tax Return Preparers** lists credentialed tax professionals by location. In California, tax preparers must be registered with the **California Tax Education Council (CTEC)**; you can verify a preparer's registration using CTEC's online directory. The IRS maintains dedicated resources for applicable large employers at the **ACA Information Center for Applicable Large Employers**, and small employers can find ACA-specific guidance at **Affordable Care Act Tax Provisions for Small Employers**.

### Note on filing deadlines

This page does **not** publish specific filing due dates for Forms 1095-C or 1094-C. For complete filing instructions, refer to a tax professional or the **IRS Form 1095-C instructions (PDF)** linked from this page.
