# Health Plans — Covered California for Small Business

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/small-business/health-plans
- **Covered California section:** For Small Business — Health Plans
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

CCSB offers health plans across all four metal coverage levels — Platinum, Gold, Silver and Bronze — from multiple insurance companies, and small businesses can enroll year-round. Employers choose which tiers to offer, but the tiers must be **adjacent** in the metal sequence: for example Bronze and Silver qualify, but Bronze and Gold do not. The page also covers Medicare creditable coverage notice obligations and links to 2025, 2026 and 2027 plan and network summaries.

## Key Facts

- Four coverage levels: **Platinum, Gold, Silver, Bronze**.
- When offering **two or three tiers, the tiers must be adjacent** — next to each other in the tier sequence. Bronze and Silver qualify; **Bronze and Gold do not**.
- Valid combinations: All four metal tiers (Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum); three touching tiers (Bronze/Silver/Gold or Silver/Gold/Platinum); two touching tiers (Bronze/Silver, Silver/Gold, Gold/Platinum); one tier of your choice.
- Employers **must provide an annual written notice** to each Medicare-eligible employee explaining whether the company's prescription drug coverage is **creditable** (covers at least as much as standard Medicare Part D).
- Plan and benefit summaries and network summaries are published for **2027, 2026 and 2025**.
- Small businesses can enroll **year-round**.

## Details

### Health plan options for employees

When you offer health insurance through CCSB, your employees can choose from plans from multiple insurance companies. Plans are organized into coverage levels — Platinum, Gold, Silver and Bronze — so employees can find one that fits their needs and budget. Review each insurance company's network to see which doctors, hospitals, and service areas are included.

### Choose from four coverage levels

As the coverage level increases, plans generally cover a **higher share of medical costs** — and employees typically pay **more in monthly premiums**. As an employer, you control your contribution amount and can offer employees a range of tier options. The coverage levels organize plans by how costs are shared between the plan and the employee; higher tiers generally mean **lower out-of-pocket costs** for employees and **higher monthly premiums**.

| Offering | Valid combinations |
| --- | --- |
| **All four metal tiers** | Bronze, Silver, Gold and Platinum |
| **Three touching tiers** | Bronze, Silver and Gold; or Silver, Gold and Platinum |
| **Two touching tiers** | Bronze and Silver; Silver and Gold; Gold and Platinum |
| **One tier of your choice** | Bronze; Silver; Gold; Platinum |

**Important:** when offering two or three tiers, the tiers must be **adjacent**, meaning they must be next to each other in the tier sequence. For example, **Bronze and Silver qualify, but Bronze and Gold do not**.

### Creditable coverage

Employers **must provide an annual written notice** to each **Medicare-eligible employee** explaining whether the company's prescription drug coverage is **creditable**, meaning it covers at least as much as standard **Medicare Part D**. This helps employees decide whether they need to enroll in a separate Medicare drug plan.

| Year | Documents |
| --- | --- |
| **2027** | 2027 What is creditable coverage? (PDF); 2027 Medicare Part D Creditable and Non-Creditable Plans (PDF) |
| **2026** | 2026 What is creditable coverage? (PDF); 2026 Medicare Part D Creditable and Non-Creditable Plans (PDF) |
| **2025** | 2025 What is creditable coverage? (PDF); 2025 Medicare Part D Creditable and Non-Creditable Plans (PDF) |

If your business has Medicare-eligible employees, you may be required to provide this annual written notice about whether your prescription drug coverage is creditable, meaning it meets Medicare Part D standards.
