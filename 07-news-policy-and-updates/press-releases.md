# Covered California Press Releases

- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13
- **Type:** News & policy brief

## Summary

This file catalogs Covered California agency press releases from October 2025 through October 2026, drawn from the official press release index at coveredca.com and the individual release pages. The releases track the run-up to and aftermath of the end of enhanced federal premium tax credits, the launch of California's state-funded premium subsidy (HCARF), 2027 rate and plan announcements, and personnel changes at the agency and its Board.

## Key Facts / Details

### 2026 releases

**Oct. 13, 2026 — Covered California for Small Business Announces Rate Change and New Carrier for 2027 Amid Record-High Enrollment**
- CCSB announced a statewide **weighted average rate change of 8.6 percent** for the 2027 plan year.
- New carrier: **Western Health Advantage**, HMO plans available in **10 counties across Northern California**.
- As of September, **82,925** Californians receive CCSB coverage — the highest in CCSB history and a **five percent** increase from 2025 to 2026 — and **9,969 small businesses** are enrolled.
- The 8.6 percent preliminary rate change is below the **9.2 percent** increase the Business Group on Health projects for large employers nationally.
- CCSB will offer plans from **four carriers** in 2027: Blue Shield of California (HMO and PPO), Kaiser Permanente (HMO), Sharp Health Plan (availability HMO plans in San Diego County), and Western Health Advantage. Consumers have **44 distinct plan options** statewide. Delta Dental of California offers HMO and PPO dental plans.

**July 21, 2026 — Covered California Rates and Plans for 2027: California Continues Fight for Health Insurance Affordability and Access**
- The single most decision-relevant release in this set. Key figures:
  - **1,785,000** Covered California enrollees as of March 2026.
  - Preliminary **weighted average rate increase of 9.9 percent** for 2027, versus a national median of **14 percent**.
  - **$300 million** appropriated through the Health Care Affordability and Reserve Fund (HCARF) for the state subsidy in 2027 — up from **$190 million** for 2026.
  - For 2027 the state credit reaches households with income **up to 200% of the federal poverty level**, equal to **$31,920 for an individual** and **$66,000 for a family of four**.
  - **More than 500,000** Californians are projected to receive the state subsidy in 2027 — roughly **30 percent** of all enrollees.
  - This opens financial help to an **additional 200,000** Californians who were not eligible in 2026 and prevents approximately **90,000** people from dropping coverage.
  - **Nearly 200,000** consumers can choose from **two** Silver-tier plans with a **$0** premium.
  - **60 percent** of enrollees will see no premium increase; **26 percent** remain eligible for **$0** premiums.
  - **12 health insurance carriers** in 2027. **92 percent** will be able to choose from three or more carriers, and **nearly 75 percent** will have four or more.
  - Carrier changes: CalOptima Health enters Region 18 (Orange County); Molina exits Regions 15 and 18, affecting approximately **1,600** enrollees.
  - Worked examples given: a Los Angeles couple with about **$38,000** in income can get a Bronze plan for **$0**; a Sacramento family of four at about **$82,000** can get a Silver plan for **less than $580 per month**; an Orange County family of three at about **$41,000** can get a Silver plan for **less than $240 per month**.
  - Open enrollment for 2027 runs **Nov. 1** through **Jan. 31, 2027**; final rates take effect **Jan. 1, 2027**.
- Source: https://www.coveredca.com/press-releases/2026/07/21/covered-california-rates-and-plans-for-2027-california-continues-fight-for-health-insurance-affordability-and-access

### 2025 releases

**Dec. 22, 2025 — Covered California Encourages All Californians to Explore Health Insurance Options Before Dec. 31 Deadline to Get Coverage for All of 2026**
- "The open-enrollment period for 2026 ends on Jan. 31, but to have insurance in place for January, consumers need to enroll by the end of the year. Covered California's over 1.9 million enrollees also have until Dec. 31 to make any changes to their coverage for next year."
- New enrollment through Dec. 20: **123,461** Californians, "marking a 30 percent decrease over the same period last year."
- Overall **1.9 million** signed up for 2026 coverage, "marking a 3 percent increase over the same period last year," including **1,816,067** renewals, "a 7 percent increase."
- "For 2026, the state has allocated **$190 million** to provide state-funded tax credits for individuals earning **up to 165 percent of the federal poverty level**." This keeps monthly premiums consistent with 2025 levels for annual income up to **$23,475 for an individual** or **$48,225 for a family of four**, and extends partial additional assistance to those earning up to **$25,823 for an individual** or **$53,048 for a family of four**.
- Source: https://www.coveredca.com/press-releases/2025/12/22/covered-california-encourages-all-californians-to-explore-health-insurance-options-before-dec-31-deadline-to-get-coverage-for-all-of-2026

**Oct. 30, 2025 — Covered California's Open Enrollment 2026: Here to Help Connect Californians to Care Despite Uncertainty Around Federal Tax Credits**
- "Without an extension of the enhanced premium tax credits, monthly premiums are projected to rise by **97 percent on average** for more than **1.7 million** Californians enrolled and receiving financial assistance through Covered California."
- "This includes more than **160,000** middle-income consumers who currently receive the federal enhanced premium tax credits but would lose eligibility in 2026."
- "While this funding offers a meaningful lifeline for the lowest-income Covered California enrollees, it cannot replace the nearly **$2.5 billion** in enhanced premium tax credits that Californians receive from the federal government annually."
- In 2025, Gov. Newsom and the Legislature appropriated **$165 million** to expand eligibility for the enhanced cost-sharing reduction program, "allowing Californians with incomes above 200 percent of the federal poverty level to enroll in an Enhanced Silver 73 plan with no deductibles and reduced out-of-pocket costs."
- "For 2026, the state has allocated **$190 million** to provide state-funded tax credits for individuals earning **up to 150 percent of the federal poverty level**." — Note this figure differs from the Dec. 22, 2025 release, which states **165 percent** for the same **$190 million** appropriation. Both are recorded here as published; the later release supersedes.
- "Nearly **92 percent** of Covered California's enrollees receive financial help. Nearly **half** qualify for health insurance costing **$10 or less per month** in 2026, and nearly **a quarter** could get a Silver plan for that same price." Also: "about **17 percent** of current consumers will pay **$0** in premiums in 2026 if they keep their current plan."
- Enrollment: 11 health insurance companies offered plans for 2026; enrollment "has increased nearly **24 percent** over the past four years."
- CalSIM modeling (UCLA Center for Health Policy Research) estimates nearly **1.2 million** Californians remain uninsured and eligible for coverage in 2026 — **508,000** eligible for Covered California subsidies and **682,000** eligible for low-cost or no-cost Medi-Cal. Among the 508,000, **57 percent** are Latino and **50 percent** are between 45 and 64.
- Source: https://www.coveredca.com/press-releases/2025/10/30/covered-californias-open-enrollment-2026-here-to-help-connect-californians-to-care-despite-uncertainty-around-federal-tax-credits
