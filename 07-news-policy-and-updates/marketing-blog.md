- **Covered California section:** Editorial / Consumer Education
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

The Covered Blog is Covered California's editorial hub — plain-language consumer education on health insurance, published separately from the enrollment pages. It is aimed at people making decisions, not just people shopping on the marketplace: articles explain the individual mandate, special enrollment, plan tiers, COBRA vs. Medi-Cal, short-term plans, and telehealth routing.

Note that the blog is **not part of the official enrollment or appeals process**. Nothing in it is a plan document, and it does not override the eligibility and benefit rules on the transactional pages.

- **Tagline:** "The Covered Blog is your go-to resource for making sense of health insurance, getting the most out of your benefits, and living your healthiest life."

### Selected article: Special Enrollment

**The core rule.** "Life doesn't wait for open enrollment." Most major life changes give you **60 days from the date the qualifying event occurred**. For events involving **loss of coverage or a move**, you can usually apply up to **60 days *before*** the change to avoid a gap. After the window closes, you must wait for the next open enrollment.

**Qualifying events named:**

- Losing job-based coverage (layoff, reduced hours)
- Losing individual coverage (plan ends, or the insurer leaves your area)
- Death of a household member affecting eligibility
- Moving to California from another state or country
- Income changes that qualify you for financial help
- Income changes that make you ineligible for Medi-Cal

The article also frames COBRA ending, welcoming a new child, and moving to a new ZIP code as triggers.

**Effective dates** depend on the event and the selection date:

| Situation | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Select a plan on Aug 13 | Coverage begins **Sept 1** (first of the following month) |
| Select before current coverage ends | New plan starts the first of the following month after the old ends; **a mid-month gap is possible** |
| Event-based retroactive eligibility | Coverage begins on the **event date**, applied retroactively once you enroll |

**Documents.** You do not need documents to window shop, compare, pick a plan, and enroll — only to complete enrollment. Typical proof: job termination letter, birth paperwork, marriage certificate; lease, mortgage, recent utility bill, official mail; recent pay stubs, self-employment records, unemployment benefits letter, recent tax returns. If something is missing, Covered California will notify you.

*Note the discrepancy worth watching:* the enrollment pages state a **90-day** SEP window after losing **Medi-Cal** coverage, whereas this article generically says "typically 60 days." The specific rule governs — see `03-enrollment-and-eligibility/losing-medi-cal-coverage.md`.

### Selected article: The Individual Mandate

California is one of **5 states (plus Washington, D.C.)** to have implemented its own individual mandate, beginning in **2020**. The ACA passed in 2010 established both pre-existing condition protections and a federal individual mandate. The federal penalty was **zeroed out for 2019**, after which costs were "on track to spiral higher and higher" — the stated justification for California's own mandate.

The rationales given for the state mandate are the same as the federal one: broader participation lowers costs for everyone, and uninsured emergency-room costs are otherwise shifted to everyone else and to taxpayers. California defines **minimum essential coverage the same way** federal law does.

**Caveat:** this article is editorial background. The operative penalty amounts, exemptions, and affordability test are on `01-coverage-and-plans/tax-penalty-details-and-exemptions.md` (minimum **$950** per adult untaxed for a full year; **2026 affordability threshold 8.05%** of projected household income).
