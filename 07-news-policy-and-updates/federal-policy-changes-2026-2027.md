# Federal Policy Changes 2026-2027

- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13
- **Type:** News & policy brief

## Summary

This brief tracks the federal changes that determine what Covered California consumers pay, when they can enroll, and who qualifies for premium tax credits. The core sequence is: enhanced premium tax credits enacted in 2021 expired after Dec. 31, 2025; HHS's 2025 Marketplace Integrity and Affordability Rule attempted to restrict enrollment, verification and open enrollment; and on June 12, 2026 the U.S. District Court for the District of Maryland vacated eight of those provisions in *City of Columbus v. Kennedy*. Covered California's own consumer tracker adds that a narrowing of financial help for immigrants takes effect Jan. 1, 2027, and that full repayment of excess subsidies begins with the 2026 tax year.

## Key Facts / Details

### 1. End of enhanced (subsidized) federal premium tax credits after 2025

- Covered California's own statement: "Before, many people had extra financial help through Enhanced Premium Tax Credits, but that help ended on **Dec. 31, 2025**." ([Important Changes](https://www.coveredca.com/important-changes))
- Covered California projected the premium impact: "Without an extension of the enhanced premium tax credits, monthly premiums are projected to rise by **97 percent on average** for more than **1.7 million** Californians enrolled and receiving financial assistance through Covered California. This includes more than **160,000** middle-income consumers who currently receive the federal enhanced premium tax credits but would lose eligibility in 2026." (Oct. 30, 2025 release)
- Scale of the federal subsidy at risk: "it cannot replace the nearly **$2.5 billion** in enhanced premium tax credits that Californians receive from the federal government annually." (Oct. 30, 2025 release)
- Residual availability after sunset: Covered California's APTC band remains published as **100% FPL to 400% FPL**, available with any plan coverage level. (Financial Help page)
- Affordable options persist: "Monthly costs may be higher now, but some plans still cost as little as **$10 per month**, depending on your plan choice and income." (Important Changes)
- **Federal primary context:** the Congressional Research Service published *Enhanced Premium Tax Credit Expiration: Frequently Asked Questions* (R48290) covering the statutory sunset. https://www.congress.gov/crs_external_products/R/PDF/R48290/R48290.4.pdf

### 2. The 2025 CMS Marketplace Integrity and Affordability Rule

- Official title: "Patient Protection and Affordable Care Act; Marketplace Integrity and Affordability," **90 Fed. Reg. 27074 (June 25, 2025)**.
- CMS fact sheet: https://www.cms.gov/newsroom/fact-sheets/2025-marketplace-integrity-and-affordability-final-rule (published June 20, 2025; modified June 23, 2025)
- The rule's stated purpose was to reduce "waste, fraud and abuse" by strengthening exchange eligibility and enrollment systems. Many provisions were scheduled to take effect **August 25, 2025**.
- Key policies in the rule as summarized by CMS:
  - Removal of DACA recipients from the "lawfully present" definition.
  - One-year failure-to-reconcile policy making individuals ineligible for APTC.
  - Changes to open enrollment dates.
  - Elimination of the special enrollment period for households at or below 150% FPL.
  - Changes to de minimis ranges.
  - Sex-trait modification procedures.
  - A **$5 monthly premium** imposed on certain non-confirming automatic re-enrollees (federal platform only, sunsetting at the end of the 2026 plan year).
- **Litigation and stay:** plaintiffs (three U.S. cities, a coalition of doctors, and a small-business interest group) sued HHS under the Administrative Procedure Act. On **August 22, 2025**, the District of Maryland stayed several key provisions pending final judgment. Source: https://baldwin.com/compliance/district-court-vacates-key-portions-of-aca-marketplace-integrity-rule/
- **Shortened open enrollment for plan year 2027:** the rule would have required open enrollment to begin no later than **November 1** and end no later than **December 31** each year. CMS's own deployment deck states that "Open Enrollment (OE) for Plan Year (PY) 2026 will run from November 1, 2025, through **January 15, 2026**" — note this is the federal platform date, and **California has not adopted it**: Covered California has consistently run its own window through Jan. 31. Source: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/columbus-kennedy-impacts.pdf

### 3. Restriction of APTC eligibility for lawfully present immigrants

- **Covered California's stated position:** "Only certain immigrant groups, like lawful permanent residents (green card holders), will be eligible for financial help to help pay for their insurance." **Effective Date: Jan. 1, 2027.** (https://www.coveredca.com/important-changes)
- **Covered California's immigration eligibility page** states: "Starting in 2027, only certain groups — such as green card holders — will be eligible for financial help. President Trump signed H.R. 1 (One Big Beautiful Bill Act)..." Source: https://www.coveredca.com/enrollment/eligibility/immigrants
- **Federal statutory basis:** H.R. 1, the One Big Beautiful Bill Act, restricts premium tax credit eligibility for lawfully present immigrants who have not attained lawful permanent resident status. The AMA has published a summary of changes to ACA marketplace eligibility and access for immigrants: https://www.ama-assn.org/system/files/aca-immigrant-impact-summary.pdf
- **Important nuance on dating:** the Covered California pages use a **Jan. 1, 2027** effective date for the marketplace-facing change, while the underlying statutory provision operates on taxable years beginning after 2025. Consumers should rely on the Covered California date for marketplace planning and the statute for tax-return planning.
- **Other immigration-related dates from Covered California:**
  - "As of **August 2025**, DACA recipients are no longer eligible" for Medi-Cal under the federal rule change.
  - "Starting **January 1, 2026**, adults without qualifying immigration status can no longer enroll in full Medi-Cal."
  - A new public charge rule begins in **September 2026**.
  - A **December 2025** court ruling affected state data sharing with federal immigration authorities.
- Note also that Covered California identifies **H.R. 1** Medicaid/work requirements (a 40-hour monthly work requirement is referenced in the immigration eligibility material and was on the September 17, 2026 Board agenda as "Overview of HR 1 Medicaid Requirements").

### 4. The June 12, 2026 ruling in City of Columbus v. Kennedy (D. Md.)

- **Case:** *City of Columbus, et al. v. Robert F. Kennedy, Jr., et al.*, No. **1:25-cv-02114-BAH** (D. Md.), Judge Brendan A. Hurson.
- **Order:** "the 12th day of June, 2026" — filed as ECF Document 74 on 06/12/26.
- **Disposition:** Plaintiffs' motion for summary judgment **GRANTED in part and DENIED in part**; Defendants' motion for summary judgment **GRANTED in part and DENIED in part**.
- **Legal basis:** the order vacates provisions of the final rule "pursuant to **5 U.S.C. 706**," finding HHS lacked authority under the APA to adopt them.

**Provisions VACATED (eight):**

| # | Vacated provision | Citations given in the order |
| --- | --- | --- |
| a | The $5 premium penalty on automatic re-enrollees | addition of 45 C.F.R. 155.335(a)(3) and (n); revisions to 155.3300 |
| b | Revocation of guaranteed insurance coverage for individuals with past-due premiums | revisions to 45 C.F.R. 147.104(i) |
| c | The failure-to-reconcile policy (APTC ineligibility for failing to file and reconcile prior-year APTC) | 45 C.F.R. 155.305(f)(4), including amendments adding (f)(4)(iii) |
| d | The imposition of eligibility verification for the special enrollment period | revisions to 45 C.F.R. 155.420(g) |
| e | The imposition of a shortened open enrollment period beginning in 2027 | revisions to 45 C.F.R. 155.410(e) and (f) |
| f | The elimination of the 60-day extension of time to resolve household income data inconsistencies | removal of 45 C.F.R. 155.315(f)(7); revisions to 155.320(c)(5) |
| g | The requirement that Exchanges verify household income inconsistencies when a tax filer's attested projected income differs from "trusted data sources" | revisions to 45 C.F.R. 155.320(c)(3)(iii); addition of 155.320(c)(3)(vi)(C)(2) |
| h | The changes to the de minimis ranges for actuarial value calculations | revisions to 45 C.F.R. 156.140(c), 156.200(b)(3), 156.400 |

**Provision NOT vacated:** "The following provision is not vacated by the Court's ruling and may take effect according to its terms: a. The change to the measure for calculating the premium adjustment percentage set forth in **90 Fed. Reg. 27,166 through 27,178**."

- **Why the surviving provision matters to consumers:** independent analysis concludes the court upheld the rule's premium adjustment methodology, "which is used to establish several ACA parameters, including the maximum annual limit on cost sharing. This revision will likely increase the maximum annual limitation on cost sharing and net premiums for enrollees with incomes under 400% of the federal poverty level ('FPL')." (https://baldwin.com/compliance/district-court-vacates-key-portions-of-aca-marketplace-integrity-rule/)
- **CMS response (Columbus II):** CMS subsequently issued the "HHS Notice of Benefit and Payment Parameters for 2027; Basic Health Program" final rule, effective **July 20, 2026**, which re-adresses similar provisions — pre-enrollment SEP verification, income verification when data sources indicate income below 100% FPL, and income verification when tax data is unavailable. The same plaintiffs filed a new suit, and on **July 16, 2026** the court stayed several key provisions of that 2027 rule pending a final ruling on the merits. (https://baldwin.com/compliance/district-court-vacates-key-portions-of-aca-marketplace-integrity-rule/)
- **CMS's own account of the litigation** (dated "As of September 19, 2025," therefore predating the June 2026 ruling) describes the earlier stay of "certain provisions" and lists provisions in effect as of August 25, 2025: reinstatement of the previous "lawfully present" definition to exclude DACA; evidentiary standards for termination of agent/broker/web broker Exchange Agreements for cause; removal of the automatic 60-day extension to resolve income inconsistency; elimination of gross-premium-percentage-based and fixed-dollar premium payment thresholds; and removal of the monthly special enrollment period. Source: https://www.cms.gov/files/document/columbus-kennedy-impacts.pdf

### 5. Repayment and income verification

- "Note that because of new federal rules, there's no repayment limit starting with tax year 2026. That means that you must pay back the full amount of excess financial help (APTC) you got. This amount is added to your total tax liability, which could reduce your tax refund or increase what you owe." (Financial Help page)
- Covered California: "Beginning with the 2026 tax year (filed in 2027), people who get too much financial help may need to pay back the full amount they owe. It will no longer be capped based on income." with **Effective Date: Jan. 1, 2028**; "You'll need to prove that you qualify for financial help before you get it. Financial help won't be applied while your information is being reviewed or updated."; and "If you don't confirm or update your information, you won't be automatically re-enrolled with financial help for the next plan year." (Important Changes)
- Covered California's standing caveat: "Federal decisions may affect health insurance costs, enrollment timelines and eligibility in 2026 and future years. This information (including effective dates) may change based on future court decisions." (Important Changes)
- IRS operational guidance on premium tax credits: https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/irs-updates-frequently-asked-questions-on-the-premium-tax-credit

### 6. California's response

- For 2026 the state allocated **$190 million** for state-funded premium tax credits (HCARF).
- For 2027 the state appropriated **$300 million**, reaching households with income **up to 200% FPL** (**$31,920** individual / **$66,000** family of four). **More than 500,000** Californians are projected to receive the subsidy — about **30 percent** of enrollees — with **nearly 200,000** able to choose from **two** Silver-tier plans at a **$0** premium.
- In 2025 the state appropriated **$165 million** for the enhanced cost-sharing reduction program, allowing Californians with incomes **above 200 percent FPL** to enroll in an Enhanced Silver 73 plan with no deductibles.
- Separately, Covered California notes: "Bronze plans and the minimum coverage (catastrophic) plan through Covered California now count as high-deductible health plans (HDHPs) that let you contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA)."
- And: "While federal rules around gender-affirming care under the Affordable Care Act have changed, California law requires all Covered California plans to continue covering these services."
- Source: https://www.coveredca.com/press-releases/2026/07/21/covered-california-rates-and-plans-for-2027-california-continues-fight-for-health-insurance-affordability-and-access
