## Summary

This page walks an enrollee through the first steps after enrolling: paying the first bill, confirming doctors and pharmacies are in network, getting an ID card, scheduling care, filling prescriptions and using free preventive care. It sets out the essential health benefits every plan covers from day one, and stresses that seeing an out-of-network provider can mean paying the full cost yourself.

## Key Facts

- Every plan includes the **same essential health benefits**, covered **from day one**.
- Pediatric care includes **dental and vision for children**; adult dental and vision are **not** included.
- **Your plan starts once you pay your first bill** — pay your health insurance company, not Covered California.
- **If your plan is canceled for non-payment, you'll owe the full cost of any care you received.**
- "Next Steps: Get Ready to Use Your Plan" is published as four cards: **Make Your First Payment**, **Check Your Providers**, **Get Care**, and **Watch for Mail**.
- Your plan begins the **first day of the month after you enroll and pay your first monthly premium**. Example: enrolling **Dec. 15** means coverage starts **Jan. 1**.
- A major life change can produce a **faster start date** — a new baby's coverage can start on their **date of birth**.
- Seeing an **out-of-network** provider can mean paying the **full cost** yourself; check your plan directory first.
- You can **change your assigned primary care physician at any time** by contacting your insurance company.
- Some plans require a **referral** before seeing a specialist.
- Preventive care is free **if you tell the provider it is a preventive or wellness visit**; discussing a new health concern may convert the visit to a regular visit with a copay.
- Your insurer sends your **ID card after receiving your first payment**.
- If you keep the same plan and insurer at renewal you can **keep your current ID card**; switching plans or insurers means a **new card and welcome packet after you pay**.

## Details

### What Is Included in Your Plan

Every plan includes the same essential health benefits, no matter which one you have. These services are covered **from day one**:

- Outpatient care (care you get without staying overnight in a hospital)
- Emergency services
- Hospital care, including surgery and overnight stays
- Pregnancy, maternity, and newborn care, before and after birth
- Mental health and substance use treatment, including counseling and therapy
- Prescription drugs
- Rehabilitation care and equipment
- Lab tests
- Preventive care, wellness visits, and chronic condition management
- Pediatric care, including dental and vision for children (adult dental and vision are **not** included)

This is a general overview. Contact your health insurance company to learn more about your plan's benefits. What you pay for these services can vary by plan.

### Make Your First Payment

Your plan starts once you pay your first bill. If you did not pay when you enrolled, make your first payment to your **health insurance company (not Covered California)** to start using your plan.

### Check Your Providers

Make sure your doctors, specialists and pharmacies are in your plan's network. Seeing an out-of-network provider can mean paying the full cost yourself — check your plan's directory first.

### When Your Plan Starts

Your plan begins on the **first day of the month after you enroll and pay your first monthly premium**. For example, if you enroll on **Dec. 15**, your coverage starts **Jan. 1**.

If you have a major life change — having a baby, getting married, or losing other insurance — you may qualify for a faster start date even during open enrollment. For example, a new baby's coverage can start on their **date of birth** rather than the first day of the next month.

### Choose Your Doctor

Your doctor (primary care physician, or PCP) is the person you usually see for checkups and most health concerns. Your insurer may match you with one after you enroll. You do not have to keep the doctor you are assigned — you can change at any time by contacting your insurance company.

### Get Care When You Need It

- Contact your doctor's office to schedule a visit for routine checkups, ongoing health concerns or follow-up care.
- **Specialists:** some plans require a referral first — check with your insurance company before booking.
- For urgent but non-emergency care, many plans offer urgent care or telehealth options. Check your plan materials or contact your insurance company.

### Use Your Free Preventive Care

Most plans cover preventive care for free, including yearly checkups, screenings and vaccines.

**Important:** Tell your provider you want a **preventive or wellness visit** to keep it free. If you discuss a new health concern, the visit may be considered a regular visit instead, and you may receive a bill for your copay or related charges.

Detailed lists of covered preventive services are published on three separate pages — Free preventive care for adults, Free preventive care for children, and Free preventive care for women — captured in this reference library.

### In an Emergency

If you think you are having a medical emergency, call **911** or go to the nearest hospital. Your plan covers emergency care. Afterward you will have a copay for the visit, and the amount depends on your coverage level.

### Fill Your Prescriptions

- Bring the prescription to a pharmacy in your plan's network; your insurance company can help you find one.
- At the pharmacy you may need your **member ID card or plan ID number**.
- What you pay depends on your plan's **drug list and the drug's tier**; these can change, so check coverage before filling a prescription.

### Your Member ID Card

- Your insurance company sends your ID card **after they get your first payment**. Paying early gets you your card and plan materials sooner.
- If you keep the same plan and insurance company at renewal, you can **keep your current card**.
- If you switch plans or insurance companies, you will get a **new card and welcome packet after you pay**.
- Lost your card? Contact your insurance company for a replacement.

### If Your Insurance Changes During Treatment

If your new plan does not include your current doctor and you are being treated for a serious health condition:

1. **Call your new insurance company right away** to tell them about your treatment — they may be able to work with your current doctor while you finish treatment.
2. **Tell your current doctor** you have new insurance.
3. Contact your new insurer if you have questions about continuing your care.

### Watch for Mail

- Covered California sends a **welcome letter and brochure**.
- Your insurance company sends your **ID card and enrollment packet**.

### Get Care Right Away

**Get Care** (card)

- You can get care as soon as your plan starts — no need to wait for your ID card.
- Just make sure your first payment is processed.
- If your plan is canceled for non-payment, you'll owe the full cost of any care you received.

### Free Consumer Help

The **Health Consumer Alliance** offers free local help with insurance problems — call **(888) 804-3536** or visit **healthconsumer.org**.
