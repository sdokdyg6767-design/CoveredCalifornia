# Moving to Medicare

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/account/renew/moving-to-medicare
- **Covered California section:** Managing Your Account
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains the transition from a Covered California plan to Medicare for people approaching 65 or otherwise becoming eligible. The critical operational point is that a Covered California plan is **not** automatically cancelled when you become Medicare-eligible, even if you join a Medicare plan with the same insurer — you must cancel it yourself with at least 14 days' notice and report your Medicare eligibility within 30 days. Failing to cancel can mean repaying financial help and delaying your Medicare start date.

## Key Facts

- Covered California **does not sell Medicare plans**.
- Medicare-eligible people are generally **not eligible for financial help** to lower the monthly premium for a Covered California plan, even if they do not enroll in Medicare.
- **Report your Medicare eligibility to Covered California within 30 days.**
- You must **cancel your plan at least 14 days before** you want it to end.
- Your Covered California plan is **not automatically cancelled** when you become eligible for Medicare, even if you enroll with the same health insurance company.
- Medicare enrollment is **not automatic** unless you currently receive Social Security benefits.
- The Medicare initial enrollment period is **seven months**: it starts **three months before** your birthday month and ends **three months after**.
- Contact Social Security to confirm your Part A status and start date.
- Call Covered California at **(800) 300-1506** to report Medicare eligibility and discuss options.
- If you keep your Covered California plan while Medicare-eligible, you **may have to pay back your financial help** and your **Medicare coverage start date could be delayed**.
- Not signing up for Medicare Part B during your initial enrollment period can trigger a **lifetime penalty increasing your monthly premium by 10 percent or more**.
- The general enrollment period is **January 1 to March 31**, with insurance starting **the following July**.
- Social Security Administration disability determination triggers **automatic Medicare enrollment two years after you start getting SSDI income**.
- You can keep a Covered California plan instead of paying for Part A and may still qualify for financial help based on your income.

## Details

### What Is Medicare?

Medicare is a federal health insurance program for people who are 65 or older, certain younger people with disabilities, and people with permanent kidney failure (end-stage renal disease). It has several parts covering different services, including hospital care, medical care and prescription drugs. **Covered California does not sell Medicare plans** — for details on what each part covers, visit Medicare.gov.

### Transitioning From Covered California to Medicare

In general, people who are eligible for Medicare — even if they don't enroll in it — **are not eligible for financial help** to lower the monthly premium for a Covered California health plan.

**Medicare enrollment is not automatic** unless you currently receive Social Security benefits. If you're approaching 65, you can sign up during your seven-month initial enrollment period, which starts three months before your birthday month and ends three months after.

### If You Are Eligible for Medicare

1. **Report your Medicare eligibility to Covered California within 30 days.**
2. **Cancel your health insurance plan through Covered California.** Your plan will **not** be automatically cancelled when you become eligible for Medicare, even if you enroll in a Medicare plan with the same health insurance company. You must cancel at least **14 days** before you want it to end.
3. **Contact Social Security** to confirm your Part A status and start date.
4. **Call Covered California at (800) 300-1506** to report your Medicare eligibility and discuss your options.

Can you just let it lapse? **No.** You must call Covered California at **(800) 300-1506** to cancel your plan. Alternatively, you can keep a Covered California plan instead of paying for Part A, and you may still qualify for financial help based on your income.

### If You Do Not Cancel Your Covered California Plan

If you are eligible for Medicare and keep your Covered California plan:

- You **may have to pay back your financial help**.
- Your **Medicare coverage start date could be delayed**.
- Separately, if you do not sign up for Medicare Part B during your initial enrollment period, you may face a **lifetime penalty that increases your monthly premium by 10 percent or more**, and you would have to wait for the general enrollment period (**January 1 to March 31**), with your insurance starting **the following July**.

### If You Have a Disability

If you have been determined disabled by the Social Security Administration, you are **automatically enrolled in Medicare**. This happens **two years after you start getting SSDI income**.

- During those two years you can keep a Covered California plan. Depending on your income and family size, you may also qualify for financial help or Medi-Cal.
- If you have Medi-Cal instead, contact your local county office — they can tell you if you still qualify for programs that lower your Medicare costs.
