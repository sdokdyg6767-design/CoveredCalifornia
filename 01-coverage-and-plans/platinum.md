# Platinum Plans

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/coverage-basics/coverage-levels/platinum
- **Covered California section:** Coverage Basics / Plan Tiers
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Platinum is the highest of Covered California's four standard coverage levels: it has the highest monthly premium but the lowest out-of-pocket costs when care is received. On average the plan pays about 90 percent of covered medical expenses and the member pays about 10 percent. Platinum plans have no medical or pharmacy deductible, so covered services are available from the first day of coverage. The page publishes full out-of-pocket maximum, deductible, and per-service copay figures for both HMO and PPO/EPO plan types.

## Key Facts

- "On average, your health insurance company pays about 90 percent of covered medical expenses, and you pay about 10 percent."
- "Platinum is the highest of Covered California's coverage levels. You'll pay the highest monthly premium of any tier."
- The page carries a **plan-type card group (HMO / PPO / EPO)** under the heading "Plan types: HMO, PPO and EPO"; transcribed below under "Plan Types: HMO, PPO and EPO".
- "The biggest perk: no medical or pharmacy deductible."
- Covered services are available "from the first day your coverage begins (once you've made your first payment)."
- "Free preventive care is covered from day one."
- Out-of-pocket maximum: $5,000 individual; $10,000 family.
- Medical deductible: $0. Pharmacy deductible: $0.
- Doctor visit $15; specialist visit $30; mental health outpatient visit $15; urgent care $15.
- Emergency room $175; ambulance $150.
- Lab visit / lab tests $15; X-rays and diagnostic imaging $30.
- CT/PET scans, MRIs: $75 (HMO) or 10% of bill (PPO and EPO).
- Home health: $20 per visit (HMO) or 10% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Durable medical equipment: 10% of bill for both HMO and PPO/EPO.
- Skilled nursing care: $125 per day, up to 5 days (HMO) or 10% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Hospital facility fee: $225 per day, up to 5 days (HMO) or 10% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- The page warns: "Drug lists can change. Check your medication's tier with your health insurance company or in Shop and Compare before choosing a plan."

## Details

### Is Platinum Right for You?

Platinum "usually" has the highest monthly premium among coverage levels but the lowest out-of-pocket costs when you get care. "If you expect to use health care often, Platinum may be worth a closer look."

A Platinum plan may be a good fit if you:

- Visit the doctor or specialists often.
- Use prescriptions regularly.
- Would rather pay the most each month so your costs are lowest when you get care.

You may want to consider other coverage levels if you:

- Rarely use health care.
- Want a lower monthly premium.
- Qualify for Enhanced Silver benefits.
- Want a balance between your monthly premium and what you pay for care.

### Plan Types: HMO, PPO and EPO

"Every Platinum plan covers your care at the same level. The plan type changes how you get care and how you pay for some services. With a Platinum HMO, you pay a set amount for things like imaging, home health and hospital stays. With a Platinum EPO or PPO, you pay a percentage of the bill instead."

The page presents a plan-type card group with these three cards. Transcribed verbatim:

- **HMO Plans** — "Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies"; "Need a referral to see a specialist"; "Usually the lowest monthly premium"
- **PPO Plans** — "See doctors in or out of the network"; "No referral needed to see a specialist"; "Higher monthly premium than HMO or EPO"
- **EPO Plans** — "Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies"; "No referral needed to see a specialist"; "Costs usually land between HMO and PPO"

Note the site's wording for the EPO cost position on this page: costs "usually **land** between HMO and PPO". (The overview page at `/coverage-basics/coverage-levels` uses "**fall**" instead.)

| Plan type | Full name | Character | Key rules | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HMO | Health maintenance organization | Lower cost, more structure | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; need a referral to see a specialist; usually the lowest monthly premium | Lower costs, with a primary doctor managing your care |
| PPO | Preferred provider organization | More choice, higher cost | See doctors in or out of the network; no referral needed to see a specialist; higher monthly premium than HMO or EPO | Flexibility to choose your own doctors and hospitals |
| EPO | Exclusive provider organization | Part HMO, part PPO | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; no referral needed to see a specialist; costs usually land between HMO and PPO | Lower costs, without needing referrals |
