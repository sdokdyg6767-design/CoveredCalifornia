# Health Plan Coverage Levels

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/coverage-basics/coverage-levels
- **Covered California section:** Coverage Basics / Plan Tiers
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Covered California offers five coverage levels: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum, and the minimum coverage plan. All levels include the same essential health benefits; the difference is how costs are shared between the consumer and the health plan. The page publishes an at-a-glance table of what each tier covers as a percentage of average annual cost, explains the HMO/PPO/EPO plan-type choice made after choosing a level, and lists the essential health benefits every plan must cover. It also states that the minimum coverage plan is only available to those under 30 or who qualify for a hardship or affordability exemption.

## Key Facts

- Covered California plans come in five coverage levels: Bronze, Silver, Gold, Platinum and the minimum coverage plan.
- "All levels include the same essential health benefits."
- Cost-sharing rule of thumb: "Lower monthly premium, pay more when you get care." / "Higher monthly premium, so you pay less when you get care."
- Bronze: plan covers 60% of average annual cost, you pay 40%, preventive care free.
- Silver: plan covers 70-94% of average annual cost, you pay 6-30%, preventive care free. Footnote: "Silver coverage level depends on your income and household size."
- Gold: plan covers 80% of average annual cost, you pay 20%, preventive care free.
- Platinum: plan covers 90% of average annual cost, you pay 10%, preventive care free.
- The minimum coverage plan "is only available if you're under 30 or qualify for a hardship or affordability exemption."
- "Preventive care is always free and covered from day one."
- The page references a downloadable "Health Benefits Table (PDF)" that "compares what you pay for every covered service at each coverage level."
- Service Center: (800) 300-1506; TTY (888) 889-4500. Hours Monday to Friday 8 a.m. to 6 p.m.; closed weekends.

## Details

### How Coverage Levels Work

Every Covered California plan has a coverage level, and every level includes the same essential health benefits. What changes is the split of costs. The page explicitly says "There's no single right choice. The best coverage level for you depends on your budget and how much care you expect to use."

An interactive selector asks two questions:

1. "In a typical year, how much do you expect to use your health coverage?" — options: Once or twice / A few times / Monthly or more. The prompt adds: "Think about doctor visits, prescriptions, and medical tests you might need."
2. "What is most important when it comes to costs?" — framing: "You pay for health care two ways: a monthly premium, and out-of-pocket costs when you get care." Options: Paying less each month / Somewhere in between / Paying less when I get care.

Selecting the middle answers on both questions produces a recommendation to explore Silver coverage, characterized as:

- Monthly Payment: MEDIUM ("What you pay each month for your plan")
- Deductibles: MEDIUM ("What you pay before insurance kicks in"), with the footnote "*Not all plans require that you meet your deductible before coverage begins"
- Cost of Care ("Your share of the cost when you get care"): ~30%

### Plan Types: HMO, PPO and EPO

"After choosing a coverage level, you will also choose a plan type: HMO, PPO, or EPO. Your plan type affects how you receive care, such as whether you need referrals or can see doctors outside the plan's network. It can also affect your monthly premium."

| Plan type | Full name | Character | Key rules | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HMO | Health maintenance organization | Lower cost, more structure | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; need a referral to see a specialist; usually the lowest monthly premium | Lower costs, with a primary doctor managing your care |
| PPO | Preferred provider organization | More choice, higher cost | See doctors in or out of the network; no referral needed to see a specialist; higher monthly premium than HMO or EPO | Flexibility to choose your own doctors and hospitals |
| EPO | Exclusive provider organization | Part HMO, part PPO | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; no referral needed to see a specialist; costs usually land between HMO and PPO | Lower costs without needing referrals |

### What Every Plan Covers

"No matter which plan you choose, all Covered California health plans include the same essential health benefits. What you pay for each depends on your plan."

- Outpatient care (care without a hospital stay)
- Emergency services
- Hospital care, including surgery and overnight stays
- Pregnancy, maternity, and newborn care
- Mental health and substance use services, including behavioral health treatment
- Prescription drugs
- Rehabilitation and habilitation services and devices
- Lab services
- Preventive care, wellness services, and chronic disease management
- Pediatric services, including children's dental and vision care

Note published on the page: "This is a general overview of covered services. Contact your health insurance company for details about your plan's benefits."

**Preventive care:** "Preventive care, like annual checkups and recommended screenings, is free. If you bring up a new health problem during that visit, your normal plan costs may apply to that part of care. The preventive services themselves are still free."

**Dental and vision:** "Children's dental and vision insurance are included with every plan. Adult dental and vision care are not, but you can buy them separately."

### Talk Me Through This (FAQ)

- "What if I pick a lower-level plan and then something happens?" — "Every plan sets a yearly limit on what you pay, and every plan covers emergencies. Some plans, like Bronze, cost more if you need care, but you will never be responsible for the full cost of care."
- "How do I know if I qualify for extra savings on a Silver plan?" — "When you apply, we will determine whether your household qualifies based on your household income and size. If you qualify, you will automatically see the Silver plans with extra savings available to you."
- "Can I change my plan later?" — "Yes. You can switch during open enrollment each year, or after a major life change — like moving, losing insurance or getting married."
