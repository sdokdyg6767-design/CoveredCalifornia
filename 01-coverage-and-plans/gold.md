# Gold Plans

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/coverage-basics/coverage-levels/gold
- **Covered California section:** Coverage Basics / Plan Tiers
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Gold sits between Silver and Platinum: a higher monthly premium than Bronze or Silver, but the plan covers a larger share of care — about 80 percent, with the member paying about 20 percent. Gold's defining feature is that there is no medical or pharmacy deductible, so covered services are available from the first day of coverage. The page publishes out-of-pocket maximums and per-service copays for both HMO and PPO/EPO plan types, plus prescription costs by drug tier.

## Key Facts

- "On average, your health insurance company pays about 80 percent of covered medical expenses, and you pay about 20 percent."
- The page carries a **plan-type card group (HMO / PPO / EPO)** under the heading "Plan types: HMO, PPO and EPO"; transcribed below under "Plan Types: HMO, PPO and EPO".
- "The biggest perk: no medical or pharmacy deductible."
- Covered services are available "from the first day your plan begins."
- Out-of-pocket maximum (individual): $9,200 (both HMO and PPO/EPO). Family: $18,400.
- Medical deductible $0; pharmacy deductible $0.
- Doctor visit $40; specialist visit $70; mental health outpatient visit $40; urgent care $40.
- Emergency room $350; ambulance $250.
- Lab visit / lab tests $40; X-rays and diagnostic imaging $75.
- CT/PET scans, MRIs: $75 (HMO) or 25% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Home health: $30 per visit (HMO) or 20% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Durable medical equipment: 20% of bill for both plan types.
- Skilled nursing care: $150 per day, up to 5 days (HMO) or 30% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Hospital facility fee: $375 per day, up to 5 days (HMO) or 30% of bill (PPO/EPO).
- Prescriptions: Generic (Tier 1) $18 or less; Preferred (Tier 2) $60 or less; Non-preferred (Tier 3) $85 or less; Specialty (Tier 4) 20% of bill, $250 cap per prescription.

## Details

### Is Gold Right for You?

"Gold plans cost more each month, but you'll know what you'll pay from day one. With set prices and no deductibles to meet first, Gold is a good fit if you see the doctor often or want fewer surprises when you get care."

A Gold plan may be a good fit if you:

- Visit the doctor or specialists several times a year.
- Use prescriptions regularly.
- Would rather pay more each month so your costs are lower at the doctor or pharmacy.
- Want predictable costs and no deductible.

You may want to consider other coverage levels if you:

- Rarely use health care.
- Want a lower monthly premium.
- Qualify for Enhanced Silver benefits.
- Want the lowest possible costs for care and don't mind a higher monthly premium.

### Plan Types: HMO, PPO and EPO

The page presents a plan-type card group with these three cards. Transcribed verbatim:

- **HMO Plans** — "Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies"; "Need a referral to see a specialist"; "Usually the lowest monthly premium"
- **PPO Plans** — "See doctors in or out of the network"; "No referral needed to see a specialist"; "Higher monthly premium than HMO or EPO"
- **EPO Plans** — "Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies"; "No referral needed to see a specialist"; "Costs usually land between HMO and PPO"

Note the site's wording for the EPO cost position on this page: costs "usually **land** between HMO and PPO". (The overview page at `/coverage-basics/coverage-levels` uses "**fall**" instead.)

| Plan type | Full name | Character | Key rules | Best for |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HMO | Health maintenance organization | Lower cost, more structure | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; need a referral to see a specialist; usually the lowest monthly premium | Lower costs, with a primary doctor managing your care |
| PPO | Preferred provider organization | More choice, higher cost | See doctors in or out of the network; no referral needed to see a specialist; higher monthly premium than HMO or EPO | Flexibility to choose your own doctors and hospitals |
| EPO | Exclusive provider organization | Part HMO, part PPO | Only covers in-network care, except for emergencies; no referral needed to see a specialist; costs usually land between HMO and PPO | Lower costs without needing referrals |

### What You'll Pay

"Every Gold plan covers your care at the same level. The plan type changes how you get care and how you pay for some services. With a Gold HMO, you pay a set amount for things like imaging, home health and hospital stays. With a Gold EPO or PPO, you pay a percentage of the bill instead. With Gold, there's no pharmacy deductible, so your plan helps pay for prescriptions from day one. What you pay depends on the drug's tier: generic drugs cost the least, and specialty drugs cost the most."
