# Medi-Cal for Pregnancy

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/coverage-basics/medi-cal/pregnant-individuals
- **Covered California section:** Coverage Basics / Medi-Cal
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

Pregnant Californians can qualify for one of four pregnancy-related coverage pathways: Full-Scope Medi-Cal, Medi-Cal for Pregnant Individuals, the Medi-Cal Access Program (MCAP), or Presumptive Eligibility for Pregnant Individuals. Which one applies depends on income, immigration status, and how quickly coverage is needed. All four extend through the pregnancy plus 12 months after it ends, except Presumptive Eligibility, which lasts up to 60 days and is temporary. Having a baby — but pregnancy itself — is the qualifying life event for Covered California special enrollment.

## Key Facts

- "Pregnancy and childbirth services are covered under all health insurance plans available through Covered California and Medi-Cal."
- "Pregnancy alone does not qualify you for a Covered California special-enrollment period. However, you can apply for a Covered California plan once your baby is born."
- "When you apply, answer 'yes' when asked if you are pregnant. If you don't report your pregnancy, you may miss out on insurance you are eligible for."
- Full-Scope Medi-Cal, Medi-Cal for Pregnant Individuals, and MCAP each last "Pregnancy, plus 12 months after pregnancy ends."
- Presumptive Eligibility lasts "**Up to 60 days**" and covers "Prenatal care, care related to pregnancy loss and prescription drugs related to pregnancy. It does not cover labor and delivery or other hospitalization."
- MCAP: "Comprehensive insurance at no cost, with no copays or deductibles for covered services."
- "Because Medi-Cal counts you and your baby as two people, your income limit goes up during pregnancy."
- "There is no waiting period for Medi-Cal or MCAP."
- "Your whole household can apply for or change insurance within **60 days** of your baby's birth."

## Details

### What to Know Before You Apply

"Covered California and Medi-Cal are part of the same system. When you apply through Covered California, we automatically check to see if you qualify for Medi-Cal or a related program."

Note on eligibility and enrollment timing: pregnancy alone does not trigger a Covered California special-enrollment period; after birth, "you can apply for a Covered California plan once your baby is born."

### Pregnancy Coverage Options

"The program you qualify for depends on your income, immigration status and how quickly you need health insurance."

| Program | Whom It Helps | What It Covers | How Long Insurance Lasts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Full-Scope Medi-Cal | Pregnant people who meet standard Medi-Cal eligibility requirements | Prenatal care, labor and delivery, postpartum care, dental, mental health and other medically necessary services | Pregnancy, plus 12 months after pregnancy ends |
| Medi-Cal for Pregnant Individuals | Pregnant people living in California who aren't legal residents, and who don't qualify for full-scope Medi-Cal | All medically necessary services, plus dental and mental health services | Pregnancy, plus 12 months after pregnancy ends |
| Medi-Cal Access Program (MCAP) | Pregnant people whose income is too high for other Medi-Cal programs | Comprehensive insurance at no cost, with no copays or deductibles for covered services | Pregnancy, plus 12 months after pregnancy ends |
| Presumptive Eligibility for Pregnant Individuals | Pregnant people — or those who think they may be pregnant — who need insurance right away while they wait for their Medi-Cal application to be processed | Prenatal care, care related to pregnancy loss and prescription drugs related to pregnancy. It does not cover labor and delivery or other hospitalization. | Up to 60 days |

### Questions About Pregnancy Coverage

- **How does pregnancy affect my Medi-Cal eligibility?** "Medi-Cal sets income limits based on household size. Larger households can earn more and still qualify. Because Medi-Cal counts you and your baby as two people, your income limit goes up during pregnancy. This means you may qualify now even if your income was too high before."
- **Can I apply if I'm already pregnant?** "Yes. You can apply at any time to see if you qualify for Medi-Cal, MCAP or another program. There is no waiting period for Medi-Cal or MCAP. Presumptive Eligibility for Pregnant Individuals may provide immediate temporary Medi-Cal for up to 60 days while you apply for ongoing coverage."
- **What if I need care right away?** Presumptive Eligibility for Pregnant Individuals is the route described above, providing "immediate temporary Medi-Cal for up to 60 days while you apply for ongoing coverage."
- **Does pregnancy qualify me for Covered California special enrollment?** "No. Pregnancy by itself is not a qualifying life event for a Covered California plan. However, having a baby is a qualifying life event, and your whole household may be able to enroll or change plans after your baby is born."
  - **Deadline:** within **60 days** of the event.
  - **Coverage start:** "Coverage start options may vary; Covered California will show your date after you apply." Note this is *not* the "first of the month after you choose a plan" rule that applies to marriage, divorce, domestic partnership or a death in the household — a birth has its own row and its own, less predictable start date. See `../03-enrollment-and-eligibility/major-life-changes.md`.
- **What happens to my insurance after my baby is born?** "Having a baby qualifies you for a Covered California special-enrollment period. Your whole household can apply for or change insurance within 60 days of your baby's birth. If you are still eligible for Medi-Cal or MCAP after your baby is born, staying on your current program may make more financial sense since it comes at little or no cost. When your postpartum coverage ends, you can apply for a Covered California plan at that time."
