# Penalty Details and Exemptions

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/coverage-basics/tax-penalty-details-and-exemptions
- **Covered California section:** Coverage Basics / Penalty Details and Exemptions
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

California law requires residents to have qualifying health insurance for each month of the year; going without insurance can mean a penalty on the state income tax return unless an exemption applies. The page states the minimum penalty amounts for a full year of no coverage, explains that the Franchise Tax Board (not Covered California) applies the penalty, and lays out the two distinct paths for claiming an exemption: directly on the tax return via FTB, or by applying through Covered California during the year. It details three exemptions available through Covered California: general hardship, affordability hardship, and religious conscience.

## Key Facts

- "California law requires residents to have qualifying health insurance for each month of the year."
- Minimum penalty for a full year without insurance: **$950 per adult** and **$450 per child under 18**.
- "The penalty is applied by the California Franchise Tax Board (FTB), not Covered California."
- Covered California "can't give tax advice."
- Affordability threshold for tax year 2026: "insurance is generally considered unaffordable if your costs are more than **8.05 percent** of your projected annual household income."
- Affordability hardship exemption timing: "This exemption covers future months only, through the end of the current calendar year." / "You cannot apply after **Nov. 30** for the current calendar year." / "To cover the entire next calendar year, apply at least **30 days** before the end of the current year."
- Religious conscience exemption: "Already have a federal exemption certificate number (ECN) from HealthCare.gov? You may not need to reapply through Covered California. Call (800) 300-1506 to verify whether your existing federal ECN is still valid. If you were under 21 when you received your federal ECN and are now 21 or older, you will need to reapply."
- Religious exemption eligibility references "section 1402(g)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code" and requires "An approved and signed IRS Form 4029."
- You can apply for a Covered California exemption "online, by fax or by mail."
- Applications require "Date of birth for all household members" and "Social Security number (SSN) or Individual Taxpayer Identification Number (ITIN), if you have one."

## Details

### How the Penalty Works

California residents generally must do one of the following: have qualifying health insurance; pay a penalty when they file their state income tax return; or get an exemption from the requirement to have insurance.

### How Much Is the Penalty?

The penalty for a full year without insurance is at least $950 per adult and $450 per child under 18. The page directs consumers to the FTB's Penalty Estimator for a household-specific figure.

### Two Ways to Claim a Penalty Exemption

| Standard table | Franchise Tax Board (FTB) | Covered California |
| --- | --- | --- |
| When | After the year ends when you file your state taxes | During the year, for current or future months only |
| Based On | Your actual income and situation for the prior year | Your projected household income or current circumstances |
| Who Processes It | Franchise Tax Board (FTB); no Covered California involvement | Covered California reviews and approves; issues an exemption certificate (ECN) |
| How to Claim | Complete Form FTB 3853 with your state tax return | Submit an application with supporting proof |
| Note on Affordability | If insurance was unaffordable based on your actual income, claim this exemption at tax time | If insurance is unaffordable based on your projected income, apply through Covered California during the year |

### Exemptions You Can Apply for Through Covered California

**General Hardship Exemption** — "If a hardship prevented you from getting health insurance, you may qualify." Qualifying situations include:

- Personal or family crisis: domestic violence or death of a close family member.
- Financial hardships: bankruptcy, homelessness, eviction or foreclosure in the past six months, utility shutoff, medical debt or a Medical Support Order.
- Unexpected income loss or added expenses: due to divorce; unexpected or sudden disability; or caring for an ill, disabled or aging family member.
- Natural disasters: fire, flood or human-caused disaster.
- Other circumstances: reviewed on a case-by-case basis.

What you will need to apply: date of birth for all household members; SSN or ITIN, if you have one; proof to support your hardship claim.

**Affordability Hardship Exemption** — "If health insurance available to you through a job or Covered California is unaffordable based on your projected household income." "Affordability is calculated using the lowest-cost health insurance plan available to you. For tax year 2026, insurance is generally considered unaffordable if your costs are more than 8.05 percent of your projected annual household income."

Timing:

- This exemption covers future months only, through the end of the current calendar year.
- You cannot apply after Nov. 30 for the current calendar year.
- To cover the entire next calendar year, apply at least 30 days before the end of the current year.
- If health insurance was unaffordable in the past, claim that exemption when you file your state income tax return instead.

What you will need to apply: date of birth for all household members; SSN or ITIN, if you have one; information about any offers of job-related health insurance with supporting proof; job information; expected household income with supporting proof.

**Religious Conscience Exemption** — Available "if you or any of your dependents are members of a recognized religious sect opposed to accepting public or private insurance benefits, or that relies solely on a religious method of healing."

Eligibility criteria include:

- Membership in an approved religious sect that is against accepting public benefits, including Medicare and Social Security benefits, as described in section 1402(g)(1) of the Internal Revenue Code.
- An approved and signed IRS Form 4029, or
- You rely solely on a religious method of healing, with proof that your religious sect or division does not accept medical services and that you have not received medical services in the prior tax year.

What you will need to apply: date of birth for all household members; SSN or ITIN, if you have one; name and address of your religious sect; copy of IRS Form 4029 with required signatures, if applicable; proof related to religious method of healing, if applicable.

### Using Your Exemption When You File Taxes

"When Covered California approves your exemption, your approval notice will include an exemption certificate number (ECN). Keep this notice; you will need the ECN when you file your California state income tax return." Enter the ECN on Form FTB 3853, which is also where you report health insurance information for all household members.

### Frequently Asked Questions

- **Do I need to apply for an exemption if I don't file state taxes?** "No. If you and all members of your tax household aren't required to file a state income tax return, you don't need to apply for an exemption. If you aren't sure whether you're required to file, you may want to apply for an exemption anyway."
- **How long does a Covered California exemption decision take?** "After you submit a complete exemption application, Covered California has up to **30 calendar days** to determine whether you are eligible."
- **What if some people in my household are eligible and others aren't?** "Exemptions are granted individually. Your determination notice may show that some members of your household were approved and others were denied."
- **What if I disagree with the decision?** "You can file an appeal or a complaint if you disagree with Covered California's decision about your exemption eligibility. You have **90 days** from the date of your exemption notice to request an appeal."
