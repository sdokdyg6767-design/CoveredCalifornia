# Important Changes

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/important-changes
- **Covered California section:** Important Changes
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This is Covered California's consumer-facing tracker of changes coming out of the 2025 federal health care law, organized as a list of named changes each with an effective date. It covers the end of enhanced premium tax credits and the resulting monthly premium increases, Medi-Cal eligibility and benefit changes, a narrowing of financial help for immigrants effective Jan. 1, 2027, and new income-reporting and repayment rules. It explicitly warns that effective dates may change based on future court decisions.

## Key Facts

- Preamble: "Federal decisions may affect health insurance costs, enrollment timelines and eligibility in 2026 and future years."
- **Critical caveat:** "This information (including effective dates) may change based on future court decisions."
- The page states: "Some federal rule changes may affect how much you pay and what steps you need to take to enroll. This page explains what we know right now and what you can do to stay covered."
- Get Help phone number published on the page: **(800) 300-1506**

### Monthly Premium Increases — Effective Date: Jan. 1, 2026

- "Financial help is still available."
- "Before, many people had extra financial help through Enhanced Premium Tax Credits, but that help ended on Dec. 31, 2025."
- "Monthly costs may be higher now, but some plans still cost as little as $10 per month, depending on your plan choice and income."

### Financial Help for Immigrants — Effective Date: Jan. 1, 2027

- "Only certain immigrant groups, like lawful permanent residents (green card holders), will be eligible for financial help to help pay for their insurance."
- The page links to eligibility information for immigrants for details on which groups qualify.

### Reporting Your Income

- "Your financial help is based on the income you report on your application. If that information isn't accurate, you may get more help than you qualify for."
- "Beginning with the 2026 tax year (filed in 2027), people who get too much financial help may need to pay back the full amount they owe. It will no longer be capped based on income."
- **Effective Date: Jan. 1, 2028**
- "You'll need to prove that you qualify for financial help before you get it. Financial help won't be applied while your information is being reviewed or updated."
- "If you don't confirm or update your information, you won't be automatically re-enrolled with financial help for the next plan year."

### Medi-Cal Eligibility and Benefits

- "The federal government has made changes that affect eligibility and benefits. Most Medi-Cal members won't see any changes."
- The page directs members to Medi-Cal's website for more information and to see if they may be affected.

### Health Savings Account

- "Bronze plans and the minimum coverage (catastrophic) plan through Covered California now count as high-deductible health plans (HDHPs) that let you contribute to a Health Savings Account (HSA)."

### Gender-Affirming Care

- "While federal rules around gender-affirming care under the Affordable Care Act have changed, California law requires all Covered California plans to continue covering these services."

## Details

### Context Given on the Page

Covered California frames the tracker as responding to the 2025 federal law: "The One Big Beautiful Bill Act (H.R. 1) made major changes to U.S. health care. Learn about five key changes." The page also notes consumers "may also be able to save by switching to a more affordable plan" and that Covered California will update the page as things change.

### How to Read the Dates

The four effective dates are the spine of the page, and they run in sequence: premium increases land first (Jan. 1, 2026), the immigrant eligibility narrowing follows (Jan. 1, 2027), and the income-verification and full-repayment regime follows (Jan. 1, 2028). Note the internal tension on the Reporting Your Income section: the repayment change is described as beginning "with the 2026 tax year (filed in 2027)," while the effective date printed for that section is Jan. 1, 2028 — both are reproduced above as published.

For the corresponding federal litigation and rulemaking context, including the June 12, 2026 vacatur in *City of Columbus v. Kennedy*, see `federal-policy-changes-2026-2027.md` in this library.
