# Financial Help

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/costs/financial-help
- **Covered California section:** Costs and Financial Help
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This is the authoritative Covered California page for the three types of financial help available through the marketplace: the federal Advance Premium Tax Credit (APTC), cost-sharing reductions (CSR), and the California-funded premium subsidy. It publishes the two federal eligibility thresholds that drive premium help and out-of-pocket help — APTC for households between 100% FPL and 400% FPL, and cost-sharing reductions at 250% FPL or less, included with Enhanced Silver plans. It also flags that as of tax year 2026 there is no repayment cap on excess advance premium tax credit, so consumers who receive too much help must repay the full amount.

## Key Facts

**Advance Premium Tax Credit (APTC)**
- "A federal tax credit created to help people afford health insurance."
- "Use it to lower your monthly bill, or claim it when you file your federal taxes."
- **Available if your income is between 100% FPL to 400% FPL.**
- "Available to use with any plan coverage level."

**Cost-sharing reductions (CSR)**
- "Lower out-of-pocket costs, like your deductible, copays and coinsurance."
- **Available if your income is 250% FPL or less.**
- **"Included with Enhanced Silver plans."**

**California Premium Subsidy (state subsidy)**
- "Some households get extra money to lower their premium."
- "Available for households that qualify based on income, agreement to file taxes and enrollment in a Covered California health plan."
- "Must apply for free or low-cost health insurance and meet all eligibility requirements for APTC."

**Repayment of excess help**
- "Note that because of new federal rules, there's no repayment limit starting with tax year 2026. That means that you must pay back the full amount of excess financial help (APTC) you got. This amount is added to your total tax liability, which could reduce your tax refund or increase what you owe."

**Definitions given**
- Household: "You, your spouse, and anyone you claim as a tax dependent, even if they are not applying for health insurance."
- Household income: "The total yearly income for the people in your tax household. On this page, when we say income, we mean household income."
- Federal poverty level (FPL): "A federal income guideline that is updated each year. We compare your household information to the FPL to help determine whether you qualify for Medi-Cal, premium tax credits, cost-sharing reductions or other financial help."

**What Counts as Income** — the page's own two-column list, verified against the live page 2026-10-13.

Covered California "uses your household's expected income for the year to decide what financial help you qualify for. Not every kind of money you receive counts."

| Counts — "Include these when you report your income." | Doesn't Count — "Do not include when you report your income." |
| --- | --- |
| Wages and salary | Child support |
| Tips | Gifts |
| Self-employment income | Veteran disability payments |
| Unemployment | Supplemental Security Income (SSI) |
| Social Security benefits | Workers' compensation |

## Details

### Types of Financial Help

Covered California states plainly: "How much financial help you get depends on your household size and income. If you qualify, you may get help lowering your monthly premium, your costs when you get care, or both."

| Type of help | What it does | Published eligibility rule |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Advance Premium Tax Credit (APTC) | "Use it to lower your monthly bill, or claim it when you file your federal taxes." | Income between 100% FPL and 400% FPL; available with any plan coverage level |
| Cost-sharing reductions | "Lower out-of-pocket costs, like your deductible, copays and coinsurance." | Income 250% FPL or less; included with Enhanced Silver plans |
| California Premium Subsidy (state subsidy) | "Some households get extra money to lower their premium." | Based on income, agreement to file taxes, and enrollment in a Covered California health plan; must meet all APTC eligibility requirements |

### Important gap on the state subsidy

The financial-help page describes the California Premium Subsidy (the state-funded credit sometimes referred to externally as CalHEP) only in qualitative terms. **It does not publish a percentage-of-FPL ceiling or any dollar income thresholds for the state subsidy.** Consumers needing the numeric 2027 cutoff must look to Covered California's press releases — where the state credit is described as up to 200% FPL for 2027, or to legislation and budget documents. See `../07-news-policy-and-updates/federal-policy-changes-2026-2027.md` and `press-releases.md` in this library.

### How Income Is Used

Covered California compares household income and size to the federal poverty level. The page notes that in general, the lower the income, the more help you get, and that if income is low enough a consumer may qualify for Medi-Cal.

Guidance published:

- "Use the income you expect to earn this year, not last year's income."
- "Use what your tax household expects to earn this year, not last year's income. Your tax household is you, your spouse and anyone you claim as a dependent."
- Covered California points to a federal poverty level chart (PDF) "to understand the income ranges used."
- After you apply, you may be asked to submit documents to confirm your income. If your income is hard to predict, make your best estimate and report changes during the year.
- A health care stipend from a rideshare or delivery company should be included when estimating household income.

### Eligibility Is Determined for You

"You don't have to work out your eligibility yourself. When you apply, Covered California reviews your household and tells you what financial help each person qualifies for."

### Keeping Information Current

Consumers are advised to keep income and household information up to date during the year to avoid surprises.
