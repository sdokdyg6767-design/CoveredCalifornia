# HRA Affordability Calculator

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/costs/financial-help/health-reimbursement-arrangements/hra-affordability-calculator
- **Covered California section:** Costs and Financial Help
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This is the interactive tool page for determining whether an employer's HRA affects eligibility for financial help through Covered California. It explains that only some HRA types require the tool, states what the employer notice must contain before you can use it, and lists the three determination outcomes the tool can return. It also states that affordability can change if income or HRA terms change, so the tool must be re-run.

## Key Facts

- Purpose: "Find out if your employer's HRA affects your eligibility for financial help through Covered California."
- "If you are offered certain types of HRAs, you may not qualify for a tax credit to help purchase a Covered California plan."
- "There are several types of HRAs, and only some of them require you to use this tool."
- "If your HRA requires use of this tool, you should have received a clear written notice from your employer explaining the terms of the HRA. You will need that notice, along with information to help you estimate your household income, when you are ready to use this tool."
- The employer notice should indicate one of the following:
  - "An HRA other than an Individual Coverage HRA or a Qualified Small Employer HRA"
  - "A health savings account (HSA)"
  - "A health flexible spending arrangement (FSA)"
  - "Traditional employer-sponsored health insurance"
  - "Other types of health benefits"
  - "No health benefits"
- "You have been offered one of the following HRAs that can be used to help buy a health insurance plan on your own: (ICHRA), (QSEHRA). (One of these terms will appear on the notice from your employer.)"
- "You can choose to buy a full price health plan through Covered California or directly from a health insurance company. Keep records of your HRA offer and report any changes."
- Possible outcomes published:
  - **Affordable** — "Apply for coverage through Covered California and report your HRA offer when you apply."
  - **Unaffordable** — "Apply for coverage through Covered California and report your HRA offer when you apply."
  - **Could not determine** — "Review your employer HRA notice and try again, or contact Covered California for help."
- "An affordable vs. unaffordable determination can change if your income or HRA terms change."
- "Confirm the HRA type and amounts on your employer notice, then run the tool again."

## Details

### Who Needs to Use This Tool

The page distinguishes HRAs that do and do not trigger the calculator. Only the individual-market HRA types — ICHRA and QSEHRA — require the affordability determination, because those are the HRAs that can substitute for employer-sponsored coverage and therefore affect premium tax credit eligibility.

### What You Need Before Starting

1. **A clear written notice from your employer** explaining the HRA terms (the HRA offer letter).
2. **Information to help you estimate your household income.**

### The Three Tool Outcomes

| Outcome | What the page instructs |
| --- | --- |
| HRA is affordable | "Apply for coverage through Covered California and report your HRA offer when you apply." (ICHRA — an affordable ICHRA means no tax credit) |
| HRA is unaffordable | "Apply for coverage through Covered California and report your HRA offer when you apply." (ICHRA — now eligible; QSEHRA — credit reduced by the monthly benefit) |
| Could not determine a standard affordability outcome | "Review your employer HRA notice and try again, or contact Covered California for help." |

For the QSEHRA branch the page adds: "Keep your employer notice and update Covered California if your offer changes."

### Determinations Are Not Permanent

"An affordable vs. unaffordable determination can change if your income or HRA terms change." The required follow-up step is: "Confirm the HRA type and amounts on your employer notice, then run the tool again. You can also talk with a Certified Insurance Agent or Covered California service center for next steps."

### Full-Price Coverage Is Always an Option

"You can choose to buy a full price health plan through Covered California or directly from a health insurance company." This holds regardless of the determination, and consumers are advised to keep records of their HRA offer and report any changes.
