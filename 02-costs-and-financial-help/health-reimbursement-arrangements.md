# Health Reimbursement Arrangements (HRA)

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/costs/financial-help/health-reimbursement-arrangements
- **Covered California section:** Costs and Financial Help
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains employer-funded Health Reimbursement Arrangements (HRAs), which provide tax-free reimbursement for qualified medical expenses up to a set dollar amount per year and are used to buy individual-market coverage instead of traditional employer group insurance. The central consumer point is that whether an HRA is treated as "affordable" under federal standards determines whether the household can still claim premium tax credits. It distinguishes Individual Coverage HRAs (ICHRA) from Qualified Small Employer HRAs (QSEHRA), which have opposite tax-credit consequences, and lists the employer design features that can affect subsidy eligibility.

## Key Facts

- "An HRA is an employer-funded health benefit that provides tax-free reimbursement for qualified medical expenses, up to a set dollar amount per year."
- Qualified expenses can include: monthly premiums; out-of-pocket costs such as copays and deductibles.
- "Whether you can get financial help to lower your monthly premium payment depends on if your HRA offer is considered affordable by federal standards."
- **Affordable → "You won't qualify for financial help."**
- **Unaffordable → "may qualify for financial help depending on your income and other factors."**
- "The two most common types of HRAs are the Individual Coverage Health Reimbursement Arrangement (ICHRA) and the Qualified Small Employer Health Reimbursement Arrangement (QSEHRA). Both can be used to buy a health plan through Covered California."
- **ICHRA:** "You aren't allowed a tax credit unless the ICHRA is unaffordable" or "you opt out of the ICHRA."
- **QSEHRA:** "If the QSEHRA is affordable, no tax credit is also allowed. If the QSEHRA is unaffordable, you can still qualify for tax credits, but the credit must generally be reduced by the monthly QSEHRA benefit."
- "In most cases, your employer must give you a written notice at least 90 days before the HRA plan year starts. This is called an HRA offer letter."
- If your HRA starts Jan. 1: "you can enroll in Covered California during the open-enrollment period between Nov. 1 and Jan. 31. For insurance to start on Jan. 1, enroll by Dec. 31."

## Details

### What an HRA Is and How It Differs from Job-Based Insurance

"HRAs are different from traditional health insurance through a job. Some employers offer an HRA instead of traditional group health insurance. You use HRA funds to buy your own health insurance on the individual insurance market, including through Covered California."

With an HRA you choose your own plan on the individual market rather than enrolling in your employer's plan.

### ICHRA vs. QSEHRA

| Feature | ICHRA | QSEHRA |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Tax credit rule | No tax credit allowed unless the ICHRA is unaffordable, or you opt out | If affordable, no tax credit allowed. If unaffordable, you can still qualify, but the credit must generally be reduced by the monthly QSEHRA benefit |

This is the single most consequential difference for a consumer shopping through Covered California: an ICHRA that tests as affordable ends premium tax credit eligibility outright, whereas an unaffordable QSEHRA merely reduces the credit.

### Employer Design Features That Can Affect Subsidy Eligibility

The page lists employer HRA design rules that can affect whether the HRA qualifies for premium subsidies:

- Reimbursements are tax-free.
- Employees can shop for plans that work for them.
- Can affect ability to receive premium subsidies.
- Must include all full-time employees.
- Works with other group plans.
- Can use funds for premiums and qualified medical expenses.
- Caps on reimbursement amount.
- Must maintain minimum essential coverage to get subsidies.

### The HRA Offer Letter

"In most cases, your employer must give you a written notice at least 90 days before the HRA plan year starts. This is called an HRA offer letter. Keep it with your other important documents. You will need it to determine if your HRA is affordable and to enroll in individual health insurance."

### If You Have Not Yet Enrolled in Covered California

- If your HRA starts on Jan. 1, you can enroll during open enrollment between Nov. 1 and Jan. 31; to have insurance start Jan. 1, enroll by Dec. 31.
- "If you gain access to an HRA at another time of year, you may qualify for a special-enrollment period to enroll in or change your insurance."
- "If you're already enrolled in Covered California, you don't need to change your plan." Use the calculator to confirm whether your financial help needs to be updated.
- "Choose and enroll in a plan. If you qualify for financial help, apply it to lower your monthly premium."

### Plan-Cost Terms Repeated on This Page

Premium: "The amount you pay each month for your health insurance plan." Copay: "A set amount you pay for covered services, like a doctor visit." Deductible: "The amount you pay out of pocket before your health insurance company starts paying for some covered services."
