# Employer Coverage and Financial Help

- **Source URL:** https://www.coveredca.com/costs/financial-help/employer-coverage
- **Covered California section:** Costs and Financial Help
- **Retrieved:** 2026-10-13

## Summary

This page explains how an offer of employer-sponsored insurance determines whether a household can still get financial help through Covered California. It states the two federal tests — the lowest-cost employee-only plan must cost 9.96% or less of household income, and the plan must meet minimum value standards (paying at least 60 percent of total medical costs). It also covers family affordability, children's coverage options, and health care stipends offered by rideshare and delivery apps.

## Key Facts

- "Health insurance offered through a job is called employer-sponsored insurance."
- **You do not qualify for financial help through Covered California if your job's insurance offer is BOTH:**
  - **Affordable** — "The lowest-cost, employee-only option costs 9.96% or less of your household income." The page warns: "This percentage changes every year."
  - **Meets minimum value standards** — "The plan pays at least 60 percent of the total cost of medical services and provides enough insurance for hospital and doctor services."
- "Most employer plans meet both standards. If you aren't sure, you can ask your employer to fill out our Employer Coverage Worksheet."
- "Your family may get different results." The Affordability Tool checks you and your family's insurance separately, based on what your job's plan would cost for each compared to you.
- The Affordability Tool outcome categories published on the page: "9.96% or less"; "More than 9.96%"; "No one"; "Your family, not you"; "You and family".
- "Family health insurance usually costs more than employee-only insurance, so it's common for your insurance to be affordable while your family's isn't."
- Children: "Depending on your household income, your children may qualify for Medi-Cal or, in some counties, CCHIP."
- "You can also buy a full-price plan through Covered California for your spouse and children."

## Details

### The Two-Part Test for Losing Financial Help

Covered California frames employer coverage as a two-part test. A household loses eligibility for marketplace financial help only if the employer offer is affordable **and** minimum value. The published figures are exact and both are worth recording precisely:

| Test | Published threshold |
| --- | --- |
| Affordable | Lowest-cost, employee-only option costs **9.96% or less** of household income |
| Minimum value | Plan pays at least **60 percent** of the total cost of medical services |

The page explicitly notes that the affordability percentage "changes every year," so 9.96% is the figure in effect at retrieval, not a constant.

### Individual vs. Family Affordability

Because the affordability test is applied per person against the household income, the page explains the common real-world outcome: an employee may individually be priced below the threshold while dependents are not. Consumers should run each family member through the check separately rather than assuming a single yes/no answer for the household.

### Rideshare and Delivery Drivers

The page treats app-based work as a distinct coverage situation:

- "Drivers for rideshare or delivery apps like Uber, Lyft and DoorDash in California may be eligible for a health insurance stipend where your app company helps pay for your plan."
- "Depending on your income, you may also qualify for financial help through Covered California to lower the cost of your health insurance."
- "The stipend is paid each quarter if you meet the required number of engaged hours and are enrolled in qualifying individual health insurance, such as a Covered California plan. Contact your app company to find out if you qualify."
- If you do not already have a Covered California plan, you may be able to enroll through a special enrollment period. When applying, choose the option for the relevant qualifier; if the app company asks for proof of coverage, "sign in to your Covered California account to download your proof of coverage document."
